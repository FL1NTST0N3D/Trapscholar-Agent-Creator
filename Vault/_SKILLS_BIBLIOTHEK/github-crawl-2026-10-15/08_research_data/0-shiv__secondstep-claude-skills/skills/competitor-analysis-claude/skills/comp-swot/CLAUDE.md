# Skill: SWOT Analysis (`/comp swot`)

## Purpose
Execute a rigorous SWOT analysis that goes beyond surface-level observations. This framework analyzes a competitor's internal capabilities (Strengths, Weaknesses) and external environment (Opportunities, Threats) to produce a strategic implications matrix with concrete action items. Unlike generic SWOT exercises that produce vague bullet points, this skill demands specificity, evidence, and direct strategic translation.

## When to Use
- Evaluating a specific competitor's strategic position and vulnerabilities
- Preparing for competitive encounters in sales or product strategy
- Assessing your own company's position relative to the competitive landscape
- Identifying where to attack and where to defend in market competition
- Board or leadership presentations requiring structured competitive assessment
- Due diligence on acquisition targets or partnership candidates

## Framework: Enhanced SWOT with Strategic Implications Matrix

### Phase 1: Internal Analysis (Strengths & Weaknesses)

**Strengths — What They Do Well**
Evaluate across these dimensions:
- **Product/Technology**: Feature leadership, technical architecture, UX quality, innovation velocity
- **Market Position**: Brand recognition, market share, customer loyalty, category ownership
- **Team & Talent**: Leadership quality, engineering talent, domain expertise, culture
- **Financial**: Revenue growth, profitability, funding runway, capital efficiency
- **Distribution**: Sales channels, partnerships, ecosystem, network effects
- **Data & IP**: Proprietary data assets, patents, trade secrets, algorithmic advantages
- **Customer Base**: Size, quality, concentration, expansion revenue, NRR

For each strength, assess:
- Is it **durable** (hard to replicate) or **fragile** (can be matched)?
- Is it **relevant** to where the market is heading?
- How **visible** is it to buyers during the evaluation process?

**Weaknesses — Where They Fall Short**
Evaluate across the same dimensions, looking for:
- **Product Gaps**: Missing features, technical debt, UX complaints, integration limitations
- **Market Blind Spots**: Segments they ignore, geographies they underserve, use cases they miss
- **Organizational**: High turnover, Glassdoor ratings, leadership instability, cultural issues
- **Financial**: Burn rate concerns, dependency on funding, pricing pressure, margin erosion
- **Customer Issues**: Churn patterns, support complaints, implementation failures, NPS trends
- **Operational**: Slow release cycles, reliability issues, security incidents, compliance gaps

For each weakness, assess:
- Is it **structural** (inherent to their model) or **fixable** (they could address it)?
- Is it **visible** to customers or **hidden** behind closed doors?
- Does it **matter** for the segments and use cases you compete on?

### Phase 2: External Analysis (Opportunities & Threats)

**Opportunities — External Tailwinds**
- **Market Growth**: TAM expansion, new segments emerging, geographic expansion
- **Technology Shifts**: New platforms, AI/ML capabilities, infrastructure changes
- **Regulatory Changes**: New compliance requirements that favor their approach
- **Competitive Exits**: Competitors shutting down, pivoting, or being acquired
- **Buyer Behavior**: Changing preferences, new buying patterns, consolidation trends
- **Partnership Potential**: Integration opportunities, channel partnerships, ecosystem plays

**Threats — External Headwinds**
- **New Entrants**: Startups, adjacent players, big tech encroachment
- **Market Saturation**: Commoditization, price pressure, feature parity
- **Technology Disruption**: Platform shifts that obsolete their approach
- **Regulatory Risk**: New regulations that challenge their model
- **Economic Conditions**: Budget cuts, extended sales cycles, downgrades
- **Customer Concentration**: Revenue dependency on a few large accounts
- **Talent Competition**: Key hires being poached, wage inflation in their market

### Phase 3: Strategic Implications Matrix
This is where SWOT becomes actionable. Cross-reference each quadrant pair:

**SO Strategies (Strengths + Opportunities)**: How can they leverage strengths to capture opportunities? These represent their highest-probability growth plays. YOUR COUNTER: How do you block or pre-empt these moves?

**WO Strategies (Weaknesses + Opportunities)**: Where do their weaknesses prevent them from capturing opportunities? These are YOUR openings — pursue opportunities in areas where the competitor is structurally weak.

**ST Strategies (Strengths + Threats)**: How will they use strengths to defend against threats? Understanding their defensive playbook helps you choose where to attack.

**WT Strategies (Weaknesses + Threats)**: Where are they most vulnerable? Weakness + threat combinations represent existential risks. If you can amplify these, you create maximum competitive pressure.

### Phase 4: Prioritized Action Items
For each strategic implication, generate:
- **Specific action**: What exactly to do
- **Owner**: Who should own this (product, sales, marketing, leadership)
- **Timeline**: When to execute (immediate, 30 days, 90 days, 6 months)
- **Impact**: Expected competitive impact (high/medium/low)
- **Effort**: Resources required (high/medium/low)

## Best Practices
1. Every SWOT point must have evidence — no unsupported assertions. If you cannot cite a source, mark it as "Inferred" and explain the reasoning.
2. Strengths and weaknesses are INTERNAL to the competitor. Opportunities and threats are EXTERNAL market forces. Do not confuse the two.
3. Be specific. "Good product" is not a strength. "Industry-leading NPS of 72 with 95% customer retention" is a strength.
4. The Strategic Implications Matrix is the most valuable part. The SWOT grid alone is table stakes; the cross-referencing is where insight emerges.
5. Focus on actionable items. A SWOT that does not produce specific next steps is a waste of time.
6. Reassess quarterly. SWOT is a point-in-time snapshot that decays rapidly in fast-moving markets.
