# Skill: Sales Battlecard (`/comp battlecard`)

## Purpose
Generate ready-to-use sales battlecards that arm your team with competitive positioning statements, objection responses, feature comparison tables, win themes, proof points, and strategic landmines to set. A battlecard is the sales team's cheat sheet for competitive deals — it must be concise, scannable, and immediately actionable in a live sales conversation.

## When to Use
- Sales team is regularly encountering a specific competitor in deals
- Preparing for a competitive bake-off or proof-of-concept evaluation
- Onboarding new sales reps who need rapid competitive knowledge
- A competitor has launched new features or changed pricing
- Win rate against a specific competitor has declined
- Preparing for a high-stakes competitive deal

## Framework: 7-Section Battlecard

### Section 1: Quick Overview
A 30-second snapshot of the competitor for reps who need context fast:
- **Company**: Name, HQ, founding year, funding, employee count
- **What They Sell**: One-sentence product description
- **Target Market**: Who they sell to (ICP, segments)
- **Pricing**: Starting price, model type, enterprise pricing approach
- **Market Position**: How they position themselves (category, tagline)
- **Threat Level**: Low / Medium / High — how often do you encounter them?

### Section 2: Positioning — Why Us vs Them
Create clear, defensible positioning statements:

**Our Positioning Statement**:
"For [target buyer], [our product] is the [category] that [key differentiator], unlike [competitor] which [limitation]."

**Elevator Pitch Against This Competitor** (15 seconds):
A concise verbal pitch a rep can deliver when asked "how are you different from [competitor]?"

**Three Win Themes**:
The top 3 reasons customers choose you over this competitor. Each must be:
- Specific and provable (not generic claims)
- Relevant to the buyer's decision criteria
- Supported by customer evidence or data

### Section 3: Feature Comparison
Build a comparison matrix focused on decision-relevant features:

```
| Feature Area | Us | Them | Verdict | Talk Track |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Feature 1] | [Capability] | [Capability] | Win/Lose/Tie | [What to say] |
```

Rules for the feature comparison:
- Only include features that buyers actually evaluate
- Be honest about where the competitor is stronger — reps lose credibility if the card is biased
- Include a "talk track" column with what to say for each row
- Mark areas where you win, lose, or tie

### Section 4: Objection Handling
For each common objection, provide:

**Objection**: "[What the buyer says]"
**Why They Say It**: [Context — what prompted this objection]
**Response**: "[What to say — acknowledge, reframe, redirect]"
**Proof Point**: [Evidence that supports your response]

Common objection categories:
- "They're cheaper"
- "They have [feature] and you don't"
- "They're the market leader / everyone uses them"
- "We already use them and it would be too hard to switch"
- "Their reviews are better"
- "They were recommended by [analyst/consultant]"

### Section 5: Landmines
Strategic questions reps should plant early in the evaluation process to create doubt about the competitor:

A good landmine:
- Sounds like a genuine buyer concern (not a loaded question)
- Highlights a real competitor weakness
- Is hard for the competitor to answer satisfactorily
- Shifts the evaluation criteria in your favor

Format:
```
| Landmine Question | Why It Works | Expected Competitor Response |
|---|---|---|
| "How does [competitor] handle [scenario]?" | [Their weakness here] | [They will likely say X, which reveals Y] |
```

### Section 6: Competitive Traps to Avoid
What the competitor's sales team will try against you:
- **Their landmines**: Questions they will plant about your product
- **Prepared responses**: How to handle each one
- **Feature FUD**: Features they exaggerate or misrepresent
- **Pricing tactics**: Discounting patterns, bundling tricks, contract pressure

### Section 7: Win/Loss Intelligence
Summarize patterns from past competitive deals:
- **Win rate** against this competitor (if known)
- **Common win scenarios**: Deal characteristics where you tend to win
- **Common loss scenarios**: Deal characteristics where you tend to lose
- **Key decision factors**: What tips the balance in competitive evaluations
- **Champion profile**: What type of internal champion leads to wins against this competitor

## Best Practices
1. Keep battlecards to 2 pages maximum when printed. Sales reps will not read long documents during a deal cycle.
2. Update battlecards monthly or whenever a competitor makes a significant move (pricing change, product launch, acquisition).
3. Validate win themes and objection responses with your top-performing reps who actually sell against this competitor.
4. Be honest about where you lose. A battlecard that claims you win everywhere destroys rep credibility and trust in the tool.
5. Include real customer quotes and specific data points. Generic claims ("we're better") are useless in a live sales conversation.
6. Distribute battlecards through the CRM or sales enablement platform where reps already work, not as standalone documents they will lose.
7. Track win rate changes after battlecard deployment to measure effectiveness.
