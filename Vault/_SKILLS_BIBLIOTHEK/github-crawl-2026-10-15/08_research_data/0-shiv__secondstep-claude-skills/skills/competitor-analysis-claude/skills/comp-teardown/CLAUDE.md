# Skill: Competitor Teardown (`/comp teardown`)

## Purpose
Execute a comprehensive, systematic teardown of a competitor's business. This is the foundational analysis that feeds into all other competitive intelligence skills. A proper teardown deconstructs every visible aspect of a competitor's go-to-market strategy, product offering, and market position.

## When to Use
- Entering a new market and need to understand incumbents
- A new competitor has emerged and you need rapid intelligence
- Preparing for a strategic planning session or board meeting
- Sales team is losing deals to a specific competitor and needs context
- Evaluating potential acquisition targets or partnership opportunities

## Framework: The 8-Layer Teardown

### Layer 1: Company Overview
Gather and structure the following:
- **Founded**: Year, founders, origin story
- **Headquarters**: Location, office count, remote policy
- **Funding**: Total raised, last round, valuation (if known)
- **Revenue**: ARR/revenue estimates, growth rate
- **Team Size**: Total employees, engineering vs sales ratio, recent hiring trends
- **Mission/Vision**: Stated positioning and aspirational messaging
- **Key Leadership**: CEO, CTO, CMO backgrounds and prior companies

### Layer 2: Product/Service Analysis
- **Core Offering**: What exactly do they sell? Product vs service vs platform
- **Feature Set**: Complete feature inventory organized by category
- **Product Tiers**: How is the product packaged and segmented?
- **User Experience**: Onboarding flow, learning curve, design quality
- **Integration Ecosystem**: What tools do they connect with? API availability
- **Mobile/Platform**: Web, desktop, iOS, Android availability
- **Release Cadence**: How frequently do they ship? Changelog analysis

### Layer 3: Pricing & Packaging
- **Pricing Model**: Per-seat, usage-based, flat-rate, freemium, hybrid
- **Tier Breakdown**: What's included at each level
- **Entry Price**: Lowest paid plan cost
- **Enterprise Pricing**: Custom/negotiated pricing signals
- **Free Tier**: Existence, limitations, conversion mechanics
- **Annual vs Monthly**: Discount spread between billing periods

### Layer 4: Target Market
- **ICP (Ideal Customer Profile)**: Company size, industry, role
- **Customer Segments**: SMB vs mid-market vs enterprise split
- **Use Cases**: Primary jobs-to-be-done they solve
- **Customer Logos**: Notable customers displayed on site
- **Case Studies**: Industries and outcomes highlighted
- **Geographic Focus**: Domestic vs international, language support

### Layer 5: Positioning & Messaging
- **Tagline/Headline**: Primary homepage messaging
- **Value Propositions**: Top 3-5 claimed benefits
- **Differentiation Claims**: How they say they're different
- **Category**: What category do they place themselves in?
- **Against Whom**: Do they explicitly compare against competitors?
- **Tone & Voice**: Professional, casual, technical, aspirational

### Layer 6: Marketing Channels
- **Website**: Domain authority, traffic estimates, conversion paths
- **Content Marketing**: Blog cadence, topic clusters, content types
- **Social Media**: Active platforms, posting frequency, engagement
- **Paid Advertising**: Google Ads presence, social ads, display
- **Email Marketing**: Newsletter, drip campaigns, frequency
- **Events**: Conferences, webinars, community events
- **Partnerships**: Co-marketing, integrations, affiliates
- **PR**: Press coverage, analyst mentions, awards

### Layer 7: Sales Motion
- **Sales Model**: Self-serve, sales-assisted, enterprise sales
- **Sales Team Size**: Estimated headcount from LinkedIn
- **Demo Process**: Availability, gating, trial vs demo preference
- **Sales Collateral**: Visible case studies, ROI calculators, whitepapers
- **Pricing Transparency**: Public pricing vs "contact sales"
- **Contract Terms**: Monthly, annual, multi-year commitments

### Layer 8: Strengths & Weaknesses
- **Strengths**: What they do genuinely well (product, GTM, brand)
- **Weaknesses**: Where they fall short (reviews, complaints, gaps)
- **Review Sentiment**: G2, Capterra, TrustRadius, App Store ratings and themes
- **Churn Signals**: Common complaints, feature requests, migration patterns
- **Moat Assessment**: What's defensible? Network effects, data, brand, switching costs

## Best Practices
1. Start with the competitor's website — it reveals pricing, positioning, features, and target market in one pass
2. Cross-reference claims with third-party review sites for ground truth
3. Check LinkedIn for team size, hiring patterns, and organizational structure
4. Look at their job postings to understand priorities and tech stack
5. Analyze their changelog or blog for product velocity signals
6. Never assume — mark uncertain data points explicitly
7. Focus the teardown on dimensions most relevant to the user's decision
