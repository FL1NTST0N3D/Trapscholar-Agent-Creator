# PAS Framework — Problem, Agitate, Solve

## Overview

PAS is the most powerful framework for pain-driven marketing. It works because humans are wired to avoid pain more strongly than they pursue pleasure (loss aversion). PAS copy identifies a problem the reader already has, makes it feel more urgent, then presents your solution as the relief.

This framework is especially lethal for cold audiences who don't know your brand yet. You don't need them to care about you — you just need to name the pain they already feel.

## The Framework

### 1. Problem (Name the Pain)
State the problem clearly and specifically. The reader should think "that's exactly what I'm dealing with." Vague problems get ignored. Specific problems get attention.

**Rules:**
- Name it in the reader's own words (use voice-of-customer data)
- Be specific: "Your CPA has doubled in 6 months" > "Ad costs are rising"
- Target one problem at a time — don't scatter
- Use second person: "You" not "businesses" or "marketers"
- Match the problem to the awareness level of your audience

**Problem Identification Techniques:**
- Mine reviews, Reddit threads, and support tickets for exact language
- Interview customers: "What was happening when you started looking for a solution?"
- Survey: "What's the single biggest frustration with [category]?"
- Social listening: Search "[competitor] sucks" or "[category] frustrating"
- Sales call recordings: What objections and pain points come up repeatedly?

### 2. Agitate (Twist the Knife)
This is where PAS gets its power. Don't just state the problem — make the reader FEEL it. Amplify the emotional and practical consequences. Show them what happens if they do nothing.

**Agitation Techniques:**

**Cost Amplification:**
"That $500/month in wasted ad spend? Over a year, that's $6,000. Over three years, that's $18,000 — enough to hire a part-time employee."

**Cascade of Consequences:**
"First, your CPA rises. Then your ROAS drops. Then your CFO starts asking questions. Then your budget gets cut. Then you're trying to hit the same targets with half the resources."

**Emotional Amplification:**
"Meanwhile, your competitors are scaling. They're getting the leads you should be getting. Every day you wait, they get further ahead."

**Social Consequences:**
"Your team is losing confidence. Your boss is losing patience. The board meeting is in two weeks and you don't have good numbers to show."

**Opportunity Cost:**
"Every hour you spend on manual reporting is an hour you're NOT spending on strategy, creative, or growth."

**Time Decay:**
"This problem doesn't get better with time. It compounds. The accounts that fixed this 6 months ago are now 3x ahead of where you are."

**Agitation Intensity Levels:**
- **Light** (awareness content): Acknowledge the frustration. "It's annoying, right?"
- **Medium** (consideration content): Show the cost. "Here's what it's actually costing you."
- **Heavy** (decision content): Show the stakes. "If you don't fix this now, here's what happens next."

### 3. Solve (Present the Relief)
After the reader is fully feeling the weight of their problem, present your solution. The solve should feel like a natural, inevitable relief — not a hard sell. Position your product as the bridge from pain to resolution.

**Solution Framing Techniques:**

**The Mechanism:**
"Our AI scans your ad account in 5 minutes and identifies the exact settings that are wasting your budget. No guesswork."

**The Result:**
"Average customer saves $4,200/month within the first 30 days."

**The Proof:**
"2,400+ agencies have already fixed this. Here's what they found."

**The Ease:**
"No learning curve. No long onboarding. Connect your account, get your audit, fix the leaks."

**Risk Reversal:**
"Try it free for 30 days. If you don't save at least $1,000, we'll refund every penny."

## Advanced PAS Variations

### PASO (Problem-Agitate-Solve-Outcome)
Add an **Outcome** section after the solve to paint the positive future state. "Six months from now, you'll have cut CPA by 40% and freed up $25,000 for growth."

### PASOP (Problem-Agitate-Solve-Outcome-Proof)
Add **Proof** after the outcome: case studies, testimonials, data that validates the outcome you promised.

### PAS + Objection Preemption
After the solve, proactively address the top 1-2 objections:
- "You might be thinking this sounds too good to be true..."
- "Yes, it really is free. Here's why..."

## Common PAS Mistakes

1. **Weak problem statement** — Generic problems get generic responses (none). Be specific.
2. **Under-agitating** — If you're uncomfortable, you're probably doing it right. The agitation should make the problem feel urgent and real.
3. **Over-agitating** — Don't cross into fear-mongering or manipulation. Agitate the real consequences, not invented ones.
4. **Solving too early** — Let the agitation land before jumping to the solution. The reader needs to feel the weight.
5. **Weak transition to solve** — The solve should feel like a natural relief, not a jarring pitch. "Here's the good news..." or "That's exactly why we built..."

## When to Use PAS

- Cold audiences who don't know your brand
- Pain-driven products (solutions to frustrations)
- Competitive markets where the problem is well-known
- Email subject lines and openers
- Ad copy for Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn
- Retargeting ads (they already know the problem)

## When NOT to Use PAS

- Aspirational/luxury brands (use BAB or StoryBrand)
- Products where the "problem" is a want, not a need (use AIDA)
- Audiences in crisis (use direct, compassionate tone)
- Brand awareness campaigns (use storytelling)
