# AIDA Framework — Attention, Interest, Desire, Action

## Overview

AIDA is the most widely used copywriting framework in advertising history. It maps the buyer's cognitive journey from first exposure to conversion. Every piece of copy should move the reader through these four stages in sequence.

## The Framework

### 1. Attention (Stop the Scroll)
The first job of your copy is to interrupt. If you don't earn attention in 2-3 seconds, everything else is wasted. Attention is earned through pattern interrupts, bold claims, specific numbers, or addressing a burning pain point.

**Techniques:**
- Lead with a startling statistic: "97% of Google Ads accounts waste 40%+ of their budget"
- Ask a provocative question: "What if your best salesperson worked 24/7 and never asked for a raise?"
- Make a bold promise: "Double your email open rates in 14 days"
- Use specificity: "$47,382 in pipeline from one LinkedIn post"
- Name the enemy: "Spreadsheets are killing your agency's profitability"

### 2. Interest (Build Curiosity)
Once you have attention, deepen engagement by connecting to the reader's world. Show that you understand their situation, industry, and challenges. Interest is built through relevance and empathy.

**Techniques:**
- Describe their current reality: "You're spending 3 hours a day on manual reporting..."
- Share a relatable struggle: "We talked to 200 marketing managers. 89% said the same thing..."
- Introduce a mechanism: "The 'Compound Content' method that top agencies use..."
- Drop insider knowledge: "Most marketers don't realize that Google's Quality Score..."
- Create an information gap: "There's one setting in your ad account that changes everything..."

### 3. Desire (Make Them Want It)
Transform interest into emotional want. Desire is about painting the "after" picture so vividly that the reader can feel it. Stack benefits, use social proof, and make the outcome tangible.

**Techniques:**
- Paint the transformation: "Imagine opening your laptop to find 12 qualified leads waiting..."
- Stack benefits: "Save 10 hours/week. Cut ad spend 30%. Triple your ROAS."
- Use social proof: "Join 2,400+ agencies already using this system"
- Future-pace: "Six months from now, you'll look back at this as the turning point"
- Make it tangible: "That's $4,200/month back in your pocket"

### 4. Action (Close the Deal)
Tell them exactly what to do next. Remove friction. Add urgency. Make the CTA impossible to misunderstand. One CTA per piece of copy — never more.

**Techniques:**
- Direct command: "Start your free trial now"
- Risk reversal: "Try it free for 30 days. Cancel anytime. No credit card required."
- Urgency: "Only available until Friday at midnight"
- Scarcity: "Limited to 50 accounts this quarter"
- Social momentum: "Join 2,400+ marketers who already switched"

## Channel-Specific Examples

### Google Ads RSA (Responsive Search Ads)

**Headlines (30 char max):**
```
Attention: Cut Ad Spend by 40%
Attention: Stop Wasting Ad Budget
Interest:  AI-Powered Ad Optimization
Interest:  Built for Marketing Teams
Desire:    2,400+ Agencies Trust Us
Desire:    See Results in 14 Days
Action:    Start Free Trial Today
Action:    Get Your Free Audit Now
```

**Descriptions (90 char max):**
```
A: 97% of ad accounts waste budget. Yours probably does too. Find out in 5 min.
I: Our AI analyzes 47 data points to find wasted spend your team will never catch.
D: Agencies save an average of $4,200/month. No long-term contract required.
A: Start your free 14-day trial. No credit card. Cancel anytime. Results guaranteed.
```

## AIDA Variations

### AIDCA (Add Conviction)
Insert **Conviction** between Desire and Action. Use proof, guarantees, and risk reversal to overcome last-second doubt.

### AIDAS (Add Satisfaction)
Add **Satisfaction** after Action. Confirm they made the right choice (thank you pages, onboarding emails, post-purchase content).

## Common AIDA Mistakes

1. **Skipping Attention** — Jumping straight to features. Nobody cares about features until you've earned their attention.
2. **Weak Interest** — Generic statements that could apply to any product. Interest must be specific and relevant.
3. **Features instead of Desire** — Listing what it does instead of what they GET. Benefits > features, always.
4. **Passive CTA** — "Learn more" is weak. "Start your free trial now" is strong. Be direct.
5. **Multiple CTAs** — One action per piece. Every CTA you add dilutes the others.

## When to Use AIDA

- Full-funnel ad campaigns (Google, Meta, LinkedIn)
- Email sequences (cold outreach, nurture, launch)
- Landing pages (above the fold structure)
- Video ad scripts (hook, build, close)
- Sales presentations and pitch decks

## When NOT to Use AIDA

- Technical documentation (use FAB instead)
- Crisis communication (use direct/transparent tone)
- Audiences already deep in the funnel (use CTA/objection handling)
- Brand storytelling (use StoryBrand or PASTOR)
