# FAB Framework — Features, Advantages, Benefits

## Overview

FAB translates what your product IS into what it DOES FOR the customer. Most copywriters and product teams make the fatal mistake of listing features. Nobody buys features. They buy outcomes. FAB forces you to bridge the gap between technical capability and human value.

The sequence: Feature (what it is) > Advantage (why it matters vs. alternatives) > Benefit (what the customer gets emotionally or practically).

## The Framework

### 1. Features (What It Is)
A feature is an objective, factual attribute of your product or service. Features are necessary but never sufficient. They're the starting point, not the destination.

**Examples of Features:**
- "256-bit AES encryption"
- "AI-powered analytics dashboard"
- "24/7 live chat support"
- "Built on React with 99.9% uptime SLA"
- "Integrates with 200+ tools"

### 2. Advantages (Why It Matters)
An advantage explains what the feature enables compared to alternatives. It answers: "So what? Why does this feature matter?"

**Transition phrases:**
- "Which means..."
- "So you can..."
- "Unlike [alternative]..."
- "This allows you to..."
- "Instead of [old way]..."

**Examples:**
- Feature: 256-bit AES encryption
- Advantage: "Which means your data is protected by the same security banks use — without you needing a security team to manage it."

### 3. Benefits (What They Get)
The benefit is the emotional or practical outcome for the customer. It answers: "What's in it for ME?" Benefits are always about the customer's life, work, or feelings — never about the product.

**Benefit Categories:**
- **Save time**: "Get 10 hours back every week"
- **Save money**: "Cut your software costs by 40%"
- **Make money**: "Our users see 3x ROI in the first quarter"
- **Reduce risk**: "Never worry about data breaches again"
- **Gain status**: "Join the top 5% of marketers using AI"
- **Feel confident**: "Walk into every client meeting with bulletproof data"
- **Simplify life**: "One dashboard instead of seven tabs"

## FAB Stacking

For maximum impact, stack multiple FABs in a single piece of copy. Each FAB block should address a different concern or desire:

```
[FAB 1 — Speed]
Feature: 5-minute setup
Advantage: No implementation project. No IT team required.
Benefit: Start getting insights TODAY, not next quarter.

[FAB 2 — Money]
Feature: AI budget optimization
Advantage: Finds and fixes wasted spend automatically
Benefit: Save an average of $4,200/month — that's $50K/year back in your budget.

[FAB 3 — Trust]
Feature: SOC 2 Type II certified
Advantage: Enterprise-grade security without enterprise-grade complexity
Benefit: Win bigger deals without adding security overhead to your team.
```

## Common FAB Mistakes

1. **Stopping at features** — "We have AI-powered analytics" is not a selling point. WHY should the customer care?
2. **Confusing advantages with benefits** — "Automates reporting" is an advantage. "Get your weekends back" is a benefit. Benefits are personal.
3. **Generic benefits** — "Save time and money" applies to everything. Be specific: "Save 10 hours/week and $4,200/month."
4. **Too many features** — Pick 3-5 that matter most to your audience. Feature dumps overwhelm and confuse.
5. **Feature bragging** — Nobody cares how clever your engineering team is. They care what it does for THEM.

## The "So What?" Test

For every line of copy, ask "So what?" If you can ask it, the copy isn't benefit-driven enough.

- "We use AI." — So what?
- "So we can analyze your data faster." — So what?
- "So you get insights in 5 minutes instead of 3 hours." — So what?
- "So you can spend that time on strategy that actually grows your business." — NOW we're at the benefit.

Keep asking "So what?" until you hit an emotional or practical outcome that resonates with the reader's life.

## When to Use FAB

- Product pages and feature announcements
- Comparison landing pages (us vs. competitor)
- Sales enablement materials
- Product-focused email campaigns
- Technical audiences who want specifics
- SaaS and e-commerce product descriptions

## When NOT to Use FAB

- Cold audiences who don't know they have a problem (use PAS first)
- Brand storytelling (use StoryBrand)
- Emotional campaigns (use BAB or Star-Chain-Hook)
- Short-form social media (not enough room for the full FAB stack)
