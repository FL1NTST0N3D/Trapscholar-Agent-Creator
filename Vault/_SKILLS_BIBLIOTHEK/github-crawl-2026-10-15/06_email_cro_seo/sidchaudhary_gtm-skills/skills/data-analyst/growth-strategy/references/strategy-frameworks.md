# Growth Strategy Frameworks


> **Provenance.** The numbers in this file are **pack benchmarks**, not the reader's own data, and
> most carry no named study behind them. When you state one in output, label it inline as a pack
> benchmark so nobody mistakes it for a figure derived from their business. Where a number is doing
> real work in a decision and you cannot name a source for it, write `[NEED: source]` instead of the
> number. House rule 4b covers this and binds every skill that reads this file.

Reference for strategy by business maturity, channel prioritization, growth lever hierarchy, engagement strategy archetypes, metric targets, quarterly planning, and ICE scoring for initiative prioritization.

## Strategy Frameworks by Business Maturity

### Pre-Product-Market Fit (Pre-PMF)

**Revenue:** $0 - $500K ARR
**Team:** 1-10 people
**Stage:** Finding repeatable value delivery

| Priority | Strategy | Detail |
|----------|----------|--------|
| 1 | Focus on activation + retention | If users who activate do not retain, nothing else matters. Fix the product. |
| 2 | Skip paid acquisition | Paid channels are wasteful before PMF. Every dollar should go to product and learning. |
| 3 | Optimize onboarding funnel | Reduce time-to-value. Measure activation rate obsessively. |
| 4 | Target the 40% threshold | Survey: "How would you feel if you could no longer use this product?" You need 40%+ saying "very disappointed." |
| 5 | Do things that do not scale | Manual onboarding, personal sales, white-glove support. Learn from every user. |

**Key metrics:**
| Metric | Target | Why |
|--------|--------|-----|
| Activation rate | > 40% | Users must experience value quickly |
| Week 1 retention | > 60% | Activated users must come back |
| PMF survey ("very disappointed") | > 40% | The Sean Ellis benchmark |
| NPS | > 30 | Directional signal of value delivery |
| Qualitative feedback velocity | 10+ conversations/week | Learn constantly |

**What NOT to do:**
- Do not invest in brand marketing
- Do not build referral programs (nothing to refer if product is not sticky)
- Do not hire sales reps (founders should sell)
- Do not optimize pricing (too early to know value)

### Early Growth

**Revenue:** $500K - $5M ARR
**Team:** 10-50 people
**Stage:** Scaling what works, finding additional channels

| Priority | Strategy | Detail |
|----------|----------|--------|
| 1 | Expand acquisition channels | Found a channel that works? Add 1-2 more. Diversify before one channel saturates. |
| 2 | Build referral loops | Now that the product is sticky, incentivize word-of-mouth. |
| 3 | Invest in content/SEO | Long-term organic growth starts here. Build the content engine. |
| 4 | Target 3x pipeline coverage | For sales-led: build enough pipeline to consistently hit targets. |
| 5 | Formalize sales process | Document the playbook. Hire the first dedicated sales reps. |
| 6 | Implement lead scoring | Distinguish high-intent leads from tire-kickers. Route smartly. |

**Key metrics:**
| Metric | Target | Why |
|--------|--------|-----|
| MRR growth rate | > 15% month-over-month | Validates scaling trajectory |
| CAC payback | < 18 months | Unit economics must be viable |
| Net revenue retention | > 100% | Existing customers should grow |
| Pipeline coverage | 3x target | Ensures predictable revenue |
| Channel diversification | No channel > 60% of revenue | Reduce dependency risk |

### Scaling

**Revenue:** $5M - $50M ARR
**Team:** 50-300 people
**Stage:** Optimizing unit economics, expanding market

| Priority | Strategy | Detail |
|----------|----------|--------|
| 1 | Optimize unit economics | CAC payback < 12 months. Prove that growth is efficient, not just fast. |
| 2 | Segment and personalize | One-size-fits-all breaks at scale. Build segment-specific strategies. |
| 3 | Expand to enterprise | Move upmarket for larger deals and lower logo churn. |
| 4 | Target > 100% NRR | Net dollar retention above 100% means you can grow even without new customers. |
| 5 | Build the data infrastructure | Analytics, experimentation, attribution, make decisions with data. |
| 6 | International expansion | If product has global applicability, enter new geographies. |

**Key metrics:**
| Metric | Target | Why |
|--------|--------|-----|
| CAC payback | < 12 months | Efficient growth capital deployment |
| Net revenue retention | > 110% | Expansion outpaces contraction |
| Gross margin | > 70% | SaaS-grade margins |
| Logo churn | < 10% annually | Retain the customer base |
| Magic number | > 0.75 | Sales efficiency metric: net new ARR / S&M spend |

### Mature

**Revenue:** $50M+ ARR
**Team:** 300+ people
**Stage:** Defending market position, maximizing efficiency

| Priority | Strategy | Detail |
|----------|----------|--------|
| 1 | Defend retention | At scale, retention is the growth lever. Small churn improvements have massive revenue impact. |
| 2 | Maximize LTV | Expand product surface area within existing customers. Cross-sell, upsell, platform play. |
| 3 | Explore new products/markets | Adjacent products, new verticals, platform ecosystems. |
| 4 | Target < 5% logo churn | At this scale, each churned logo is a significant revenue loss. |
| 5 | Operational excellence | Efficiency, automation, process optimization across all functions. |
| 6 | Build moats | Network effects, switching costs, data advantages, integrations ecosystem. |

**Key metrics:**
| Metric | Target | Why |
|--------|--------|-----|
| Logo churn | < 5% annually | Defend the base |
| NRR | > 120% | World-class expansion |
| Rule of 40 | Revenue growth % + profit margin % > 40% | Balanced growth and profitability |
| CAC payback | < 12 months | Capital efficiency at scale |
| Free cash flow margin | > 15% | Sustainable business model |

## Channel Prioritization Matrix

Rate each channel on three dimensions (1-5 scale), then calculate priority score.

| Dimension | Definition | Rating Guide |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| **Cost to Acquire** | How expensive is a customer from this channel? | 1 = very expensive, 5 = very cheap |
| **Time to Payback** | How quickly does the investment generate returns? | 1 = very slow (12+ months), 5 = very fast (< 1 month) |
| **Scalability** | How much volume can this channel deliver at consistent quality? | 1 = very limited, 5 = near-unlimited scale |

### Priority Score = Cost × Time × Scalability

### Example Channel Evaluation

| Channel | Cost (1-5) | Time (1-5) | Scale (1-5) | Score | Priority |
|---------|-----------|-----------|-----------|-------|----------|
| Organic/SEO | 4 | 1 | 5 | 20 | Long-term investment |
| Content marketing | 4 | 2 | 4 | 32 | High priority |
| Referral/WOM | 5 | 3 | 3 | 45 | Very high priority |
| Paid search (Google) | 2 | 4 | 4 | 32 | High priority |
| Paid social (LinkedIn) | 2 | 3 | 4 | 24 | Medium priority |
| Outbound sales | 1 | 2 | 3 | 6 | Lower priority (but high ACV) |
| Partnerships | 3 | 1 | 4 | 12 | Long-term investment |
| Events/conferences | 2 | 2 | 2 | 8 | Selective use |
| PR/media | 3 | 1 | 2 | 6 | Brand building |
| Community | 4 | 1 | 3 | 12 | Long-term investment |

**Notes:**
- Score alone does not determine priority, strategic context matters
- Low-score channels (outbound, partnerships) may be critical for enterprise segment
- Balance short-term (high time score) and long-term (high scale score) channels
- Re-evaluate quarterly as channels mature and saturate

## Growth Lever Hierarchy

Ordered by impact. Fix levers from the bottom up, there is no point acquiring users who do not retain.

```
5. Revenue (Monetization)
   ↑ Maximize revenue per customer: pricing, packaging, expansion
   
4. Referral (Virality)
   ↑ Turn happy customers into growth engine
   
3. Acquisition (Top of Funnel)
   ↑ Bring new users/customers into the funnel
   
2. Activation (First Value)
   ↑ Get new users to experience core value quickly
   
1. Retention (Foundation)
   ↑ MOST IMPORTANT: Keep users coming back
```

### Why This Order Matters

| Lever | Impact of 10% Improvement | Compounding Effect |
|-------|--------------------------|-------------------|
| Retention | +10% more customers at every future point | Compounds indefinitely, the base grows |
| Activation | +10% of signups become active | Feeds retention pool |
| Acquisition | +10% more visitors/leads | Only valuable if activation and retention work |
| Referral | +10% more organic growth | Multiplier on acquisition |
| Revenue | +10% more revenue per user | Multiplier on everything above |

**Rule:** Never invest in a higher lever until the one below it is at least "good" (above median benchmarks).

## Engagement Strategy Archetypes

### Product-Led Growth (PLG)

```
Freemium/Free Trial → Onboard → Activate → Use → Expand → Refer
```

| Stage | Key Activities | Metrics |
|-------|---------------|---------|
| Freemium/Free Trial | Self-serve signup, no sales contact required | Signups, cost per signup |
| Onboard | In-product guidance, progressive disclosure, quick wins | Time to first value, setup completion |
| Activate | Users reach "aha moment", the action most correlated with retention | Activation rate, Day 1/7 retention |
| Use | Regular product usage becomes habitual | DAU/MAU, feature adoption, session depth |
| Expand | Usage grows, more seats, higher tier, more features | Expansion MRR, ARPU growth |
| Refer | Satisfied users invite colleagues and peers | Viral coefficient, referred signups |

**Best for:** Low ACV ($0-$50K), high-volume, self-serve or low-touch products
**Examples:** Slack, Notion, Figma, Zoom

### Sales-Led Growth

```
Outbound → Qualify → Demo → Negotiate → Close → Expand
```

| Stage | Key Activities | Metrics |
|-------|---------------|---------|
| Outbound | SDR prospecting, cold outreach, inbound lead follow-up | Meetings booked, pipeline created |
| Qualify | BANT/MEDDIC assessment, ICP validation | SQL rate, qualification accuracy |
| Demo | Personalized product demonstration | Demo-to-proposal rate |
| Negotiate | Pricing, terms, procurement, legal review | Negotiation duration, discount depth |
| Close | Contract signature, procurement process | Win rate, deal size, sales cycle length |
| Expand | Account management, upsell, cross-sell | NRR, expansion revenue |

**Best for:** High ACV ($50K+), complex products, enterprise buyers
**Examples:** Salesforce, Workday, Snowflake

### Community-Led Growth

```
Content → Community → Trust → Convert → Advocate
```

| Stage | Key Activities | Metrics |
|-------|---------------|---------|
| Content | Educational content, thought leadership, open-source contributions | Traffic, engagement, followers |
| Community | Build forums, Slack groups, meetups, conferences | Members, active participants, contribution rate |
| Trust | Consistent value delivery, transparent communication, authentic relationships | NPS, brand sentiment, share of voice |
| Convert | Community members become customers when ready | Community-to-customer rate, time to convert |
| Advocate | Customers become community leaders, speakers, contributors | Advocacy rate, referral revenue |

**Best for:** Developer tools, niche B2B, identity-driven products
**Examples:** HubSpot, Atlassian, dbt

### Event-Led Growth

```
Conference/Event → Booth/Session → Follow-Up → Nurture → Convert
```

| Stage | Key Activities | Metrics |
|-------|---------------|---------|
| Conference/Event | Sponsor, speak, exhibit at industry events | Attendance, booth traffic, leads scanned |
| Booth/Session | Product demos, conversations, badge scans | Leads captured, conversation quality |
| Follow-Up | Immediate post-event outreach within 48 hours | Response rate, meeting booked rate |
| Nurture | Sustained engagement with event leads | Content engagement, score progression |
| Convert | Event leads become customers | Event-attributed revenue, ROI per event |

**Best for:** Enterprise B2B, relationship-driven industries, niche verticals
**Examples:** Trade shows, user conferences, executive dinners

## Quarterly Planning Template

### Structure: 3 Bets (1 Big, 2 Medium)

Each quarter, commit to exactly 3 strategic bets:

### Big Bet (1)

The single most important initiative of the quarter. Gets the most resources and attention.

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Hypothesis** | Clear statement of what you believe will happen |
| **Success criteria** | 2-3 measurable outcomes that define success |
| **Resource allocation** | 40-50% of available capacity |
| **Owner** | Single accountable person |
| **Milestones** | Week-by-week checkpoints |
| **Risk mitigation** | What could go wrong and how you will handle it |
| **Kill criteria** | Conditions under which you abandon the bet mid-quarter |

**Example:**
> **Big Bet:** Launch self-serve onboarding to improve activation rate from 30% to 45%
> **Success criteria:** 45% activation rate, < 3 minute time-to-first-value, 20% reduction in onboarding support tickets
> **Kill criteria:** If activation rate has not improved by 5% by Week 6, pivot resources

### Medium Bets (2)

Important but smaller initiatives that support the overall strategy.

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Hypothesis** | What you believe and why |
| **Success criteria** | 1-2 measurable outcomes |
| **Resource allocation** | 20-30% of capacity each |
| **Owner** | Single accountable person |
| **Dependencies** | Any blockers or prerequisites |

**Example:**
> **Medium Bet 1:** Launch LinkedIn content program targeting VP Marketing personas
> **Success criteria:** 50 MQLs from LinkedIn, 10% MQL-to-SQL conversion
>
> **Medium Bet 2:** Build account health scoring model for CS team
> **Success criteria:** Health score predicts churn with 80% accuracy at 30-day horizon

### Quarterly Review Cadence

| Week | Activity |
|------|----------|
| Week 0 | Planning: define bets, allocate resources, set milestones |
| Week 2 | First checkpoint: are we on track? Any early signals? |
| Week 4 | Mid-quarter review: hard look at progress, adjust if needed |
| Week 6 | Kill/continue decision on lagging bets |
| Week 8 | Pre-mortem: identify risks for final push |
| Week 10 | Wrap-up: measure results, document learnings |
| Week 11-12 | Next quarter planning begins |

## ICE Scoring for Initiative Prioritization

### Framework

ICE = Impact x Confidence x Ease

Each dimension is scored 1-10:

### Impact (1-10)

How much will this move the needle on our core metric?

| Score | Definition |
|-------|-----------|
| 10 | Transformative, 2x or more improvement on core metric |
| 8-9 | Major, 50-100% improvement expected |
| 6-7 | Significant, 25-50% improvement expected |
| 4-5 | Moderate, 10-25% improvement expected |
| 2-3 | Minor, < 10% improvement expected |
| 1 | Negligible, marginal impact at best |

### Confidence (1-10)

How sure are we that the impact estimate is correct?

| Score | Definition |
|-------|-----------|
| 10 | Near-certain, strong data, proven playbook, done this before |
| 8-9 | High, good data, similar initiatives have worked |
| 6-7 | Medium, some data, reasonable hypothesis |
| 4-5 | Low-medium, limited data, educated guess |
| 2-3 | Low, mostly speculation, no direct evidence |
| 1 | Very low, pure gut feel |

### Ease (1-10)

How easy is this to implement?

| Score | Definition |
|-------|-----------|
| 10 | Trivial, can do in a day with existing tools and team |
| 8-9 | Easy, 1-2 weeks, existing team, minor complexity |
| 6-7 | Moderate, 2-4 weeks, requires some coordination |
| 4-5 | Challenging, 4-8 weeks, cross-functional, moderate complexity |
| 2-3 | Hard, 8-12 weeks, new capabilities needed, high complexity |
| 1 | Very hard, 3+ months, major investment, high uncertainty |

### Scoring Example

| Initiative | Impact | Confidence | Ease | ICE Score | Priority |
|-----------|--------|-----------|------|-----------|----------|
| Optimize onboarding flow | 8 | 7 | 6 | 336 | 1st |
| Launch referral program | 7 | 5 | 7 | 245 | 2nd |
| Build custom dashboards | 6 | 8 | 4 | 192 | 3rd |
| Enterprise pricing page | 5 | 6 | 8 | 240 | 2nd (tie) |
| International expansion | 9 | 3 | 2 | 54 | Last |

### ICE Best Practices

- Score independently, then discuss as a team to calibrate
- Re-score quarterly as confidence changes (experiments add data)
- Do not let one dimension dominate, high-impact-but-impossible ideas should not win
- Use ICE to rank, not to decide, strategic context and sequencing matter
- Track actual impact vs. predicted impact to improve future scoring accuracy
- Combine with quarterly planning: Big Bet should be highest-ICE initiative, Medium Bets next two

## Priority Dilution: The Actual Failure Mode

**Growth does not fail for lack of ideas. It fails for lack of focus.** Any strategy output that returns
a long list of good levers has made the problem worse, not better, because the constraint was never
idea supply.

The evidence runs one way:

- Organisations that invest in strategic prioritisation deliver around **40% more value** than those that
  do not.
- Companies selecting **fewer** priority initiatives are about **16% more likely** to sit in the top tier
  of their industry.
- Pursuing many initiatives at once reduces total impact rather than spreading it: the common outcome is
  succeeding at none of them.

So the deliverable is not a ranked list of everything. It is a short set of things that will actually get
done, and an explicit statement of what is being *declined* for now. A plan that declines nothing has not
prioritised.

### Score, then obey the score

Rate each lever honestly on **effort, risk and reward** on a 1-5 scale, then order by the arithmetic
rather than by what feels most urgent in the room. The point of scoring is to survive the meeting: a
number can be argued with on its inputs, while a feeling can only be overruled by seniority.

Where a lever's reward score rests on a baseline the user could not supply, say so rather than scoring it
anyway. An unscoreable lever is a research task, not a priority.

### The 70-20-10 portfolio

For teams with ongoing experiment capacity, allocate rather than pick:

| Share | Where it goes |
|---|---|
| **70%** | Core optimisation of what already works |
| **20%** | Adjacent channels or audiences |
| **10%** | Exploratory bets, high uncertainty and high upside |

This is the honest counterweight to two opposite failure modes. A team spending everything on core
optimisation grinds toward a local maximum and calls it a plateau. A team chasing new channels has no
compounding base. Naming the split makes both visible, and it makes the exploratory 10% defensible
instead of something that gets cut first whenever a quarter looks tight.

### Sequence what remains

Even a short list runs in an order. A small team running six levers at once cannot attribute any result,
so the levers that survive prioritisation still need a sequence and a stated reason for it - usually
either a dependency or the fastest read on the biggest unknown.
