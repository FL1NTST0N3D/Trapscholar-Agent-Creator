# Frameworks for B2C Leadgen About Us Page Audits

This reference file contains the CRO and aboutus-specific frameworks, principles, and applied research. Each section maps to a finding category from the main skill.

---

## CRITICAL — Visual verification before every finding

B2C leadgen aboutus pages are uniquely prone to fabricated findings because so much depends on VISUAL signals invisible to HTML:
- Photo authenticity (stock vs. authentic — only visible to eye)
- Team-photo composition and warmth
- Founder/owner facial expressions and approachability
- Embedded videos (autoplay, hero-position, sound)
- Review widgets (Google reviews, Klantenvertellen, embedded testimonials)
- Trust badges, certifications visibility
- Behind-the-scenes content, office photos
- Authentic vs. stock imagery (the entire point of the page)

**For every finding, verify against screenshots before delivering.**

---

## CRITICAL — Trust-building is the primary conversion job

The aboutus visitor is in a different state than service-page or contactpage visitors. They are NOT looking for a service or contact info — they are looking for **reasons to trust this brand**. Conversion happens later, downstream, on another page.

**Do NOT automatically recommend on aboutus:**
- "Add aggressive CTAs" — wrong page type, breaks trust-building atmosphere
- "Add lead-magnet propositie" — wrong page type, transactional pattern
- "Add scarcity / urgency" — destroys trust on a trust-building page
- "Shorten content to push to form" — content depth IS the conversion driver here
- "Add multiple competing CTAs" — visitor needs to absorb, not choose

**DO recommend (when appropriate):**
- People-first content (founder/team photos, bios, story)
- Authentic imagery over stock
- Specific verifiable claims (jaren actief, klantenaantal, certifications)
- Soft CTAs that route to relevant next-pages (service-pages, contact)
- Visual depth (photos, video, behind-the-scenes)
- Authenticity signals (real testimonials with photos, real team)

The trust-primary rule trumps direct-conversion-CTA logic on this page-type.

---

## CRITICAL — People-trust over service-trust

For B2C service-businesses, consumers select WHO they trust, not just WHAT they buy:
- "Welke kapper gaat MIJN haar knippen?" (specific person matters)
- "Welke tandarts behandelt MIJ?" (rapport with specific provider critical)
- "Welke fotograaf legt MIJN bruiloft vast?" (creative-fit + personality matters)
- "Welke makelaar verkoopt MIJN huis?" (long-term relationship trust)

This is fundamentally different from B2C ecom aboutus (where products are interchangeable). Founder/team photos and bios carry HEAVY weight here — they are the conversion drivers.

**Implications for findings:**
- Missing team photos on people-driven service business = critical finding
- Missing founder bio on solo-provider = critical finding  
- Stock team photos = critical finding (worse than no photos)
- Generic "team" page without names/roles = critical finding

---

## CRITICAL — Authenticity over polish

Trust correlates more with authentic imperfection than stock-perfect polish, ESPECIALLY for warmth-driven brands (kapsalons, fotografen, kleine zorgpraktijken).

**Authentic signals build trust:**
- Photos with imperfect framing but real expressions
- Bios with personal interests / hobbies
- Office/practice photos showing actual work environment
- Story with vulnerability (challenges overcome, why this work)
- Mix of professional and personal

**Polish signals can erode trust on warmth-driven brands:**
- Identical stock-style team-photos (everyone same pose, same lighting)
- Generic corporate bios ("X is passionate about Y")
- Stock office photos (open-plan, smiling diverse team)
- Mission-vision-values in corporate-language
- Over-produced video without personality

**Calibration:** premium-brands can support more polish (premium fotograaf, high-end advocaat). Volume/value brands need less polish. Warmth-brands need authentic imperfection.

When in doubt, lean authentic.

---

## CRITICAL — Solo-vs-team-structure calibration

Recommendations vary heavily by structure:

**Solo-provider** (1-persoon kapsalon, fotograaf, klusbedrijf, fysio):
- Founder = brand, founder = service-provider
- Persoonlijke storytelling dominant
- "Over mij" page typically (vs. "Over ons")
- Founder photo, bio, philosophy, work-history
- Reviews/testimonials are person-focused
- Risk: too-corporate language alienates audience seeking personal connection

**Klein team (2-5 mensen)** (kleine tandartspraktijk, fotografie-studio, makelaardij):
- Team-grid met named members en rollen
- Individuele expertise zichtbaar
- Persoonlijke werkrelatie communiceren
- Per-person bio (concise, 50-100 woorden)
- Per-person photo (authentic, niet stock)

**Groter team (6+ mensen)** (grotere kliniek, agentschap, klusbedrijf):
- Team-grid of-pyramide structure
- Key leiders (founder/directie) prominenter
- Departments-structuur acceptabel
- Per-person bios kunnen korter (30-50 woorden)
- Aggregate values-statement effectief

**Multi-vestiging organisatie** (kapsalon-keten, tandartsketens):
- Brand-storytelling dominant boven individuele bios
- Per-vestiging team optioneel
- Corporate-vs-lokaal balans
- Aggregate trust signals dominant

## Core frameworks (apply across the entire audit)

### Edelman Trust Barometer
The authoritative source on what builds consumer trust:
- **Faces build trust** — visible faces (founder, team) significantly outperform faceless brands
- **Specificity over generics** — "Sinds 2014" beats "Jarenlange ervaring"
- **Third-party validation** — certifications, awards, press
- **Authenticity over polish** — real beats curated for warmth-driven services
- Trust-erosion is asymmetric: hard to build, easy to lose

### StoryBrand framework (Donald Miller) — customer as hero
On aboutus, this means:
- Customer = hero with a problem
- Brand/founder = guide with expertise (NOT hero of their own story)
- Plan = how the brand helps customer succeed
- Aboutus copy should subtly position consumer at center, brand as enabler

Avoid: "Our journey started in 2012 when our founder discovered..." (brand as hero)
Better: "We help [customer-type] achieve [outcome] through [approach]. Our founder built this practice after seeing [customer pain]..." (customer at center, brand as enabler)

### Cialdini's principles on aboutus
- **Liking** — VERY heavy on aboutus. Faces, warmth, shared values, authenticity all activate liking.
- **Authority** — credentials, expertise, awards build authority-trust
- **Social proof** — testimonials with photos, customer counts, named recommendations
- **Reciprocity** — sharing knowledge/values freely builds reciprocity-pull
- **Commitment-consistency** — alignment of stated values with visible actions
- **Scarcity** — typically NOT appropriate on aboutus (transactional pattern)

### Nielsen Norman about-page UX research
Key principles:
- Visitors scan aboutus for: who, why, where, how-they-work
- Faces and names dominate first impressions
- Generic "team values" walls underperform specific story
- Page should answer: "Why should I trust these people?"

### MECLABS Conversion Sequence Heuristic on aboutus
C = 4m + 3v + 2(i−f) − 2a

On aboutus pages:
- **m (motivation):** lower than service/contact pages (visitor still evaluating)
- **v (value):** people-value, expertise-value, alignment-value
- **i (incentive):** NOT direct-action incentive; instead, "feel comfortable proceeding"
- **f (friction):** generic content, missing faces, stock photos, corporate-jargon
- **a (anxiety):** "Are these real people?", "Can I trust them?", "Will I fit?"

### Fogg Behavior Model on aboutus
Aboutus is unique because the desired BEHAVIOR is "feel confident enough to proceed" — not a direct click. The model still applies:
- MOTIVATION builds through people-connection, story-resonance, values-fit
- ABILITY is downstream (the page itself just needs to be navigable)
- TRIGGER is soft (route to relevant next-page when trust is built)

### Warmth-competence balance (Fiske/Cuddy "Stereotype Content Model")
Two dimensions consumers evaluate:
- **Warmth** (is this person friendly, trustworthy, well-meaning?)
- **Competence** (is this person capable, skilled, effective?)

B2C service-business aboutus needs BOTH. Pure warmth = "nice but amateur". Pure competence = "skilled but cold". The best aboutus-pages communicate both:
- Warmth: smile, casual language, hobbies, personality
- Competence: years experience, certifications, specialisations, named outcomes

### Jobs-to-be-Done on B2C aboutus
B2C consumers on an aboutus are doing ONE of:
1. **Trust-verification** — "Are these legit people I can trust?"
2. **Personality-fit-check** — "Would I feel comfortable with this person/team?"
3. **Expertise-verification** — "Do they have credentials/experience for my issue?"
4. **Values-alignment** — "Do their values match mine?"
5. **Pre-purchase reassurance** — "I'm about to contact them — last check"

Each job benefits from different content. Strong aboutus pages serve multiple jobs simultaneously.

**Important Dutch translation:** never use "huren" for JTBD framing. Use "kiezen voor", "zoeken om X op te lossen", "consumenten die X willen".

## Category 1: Above-the-fold brand-essence & storytelling-hook

### April Dunford positioning on aboutus
The hero must answer: **"Wie zijn deze mensen, wat geloven ze, en waarom zou ik ze vertrouwen?"** within 5-8 seconds (slightly longer than other pages — visitor accepts more time on aboutus).

### StoryBrand customer-as-hero applied to aboutus hero
Hero should hint at customer-centered narrative:
- NOT: "Welkom bij [Brand] — wij zijn al 15 jaar uw partner"
- YES: "We helpen [doelgroep] [outcome]. Sinds 2010, met [team-size] passion-driven specialists."

### Aboutus-specific hero patterns
- People-photo dominant (founder/team) is the strongest hero pattern
- Video-hero (30-60s introductie) is high-leverage for warmth-driven brands
- Quote-pull-out from founder works for solo-providers
- Avoid generic stock-imagery in aboutus hero (signals inauthenticity)

### Best practices
- Hero communicates WIE + WAT + WAAROM in 5-8 seconden
- Authentic people-photo (founder/team) dominant
- Optional founder-video for warmth-driven brands
- Hero text balances story-essence and credibility-anchor
- Avoid corporate-jargon ("excellence", "passion", "dedicated")

### Common failures
- Generic "Welkom bij..." hero
- Stock business-imagery as hero
- Mission/vision/values wall as hero (too abstract)
- Brand-history dump as hero (visitor doesn't care yet)
- No people visible in hero

### Solo-vs-team calibration
- Solo-provider: founder photo dominant, founder-first-person voice ("Ik ben...")
- Team: team-photo of founder, brand-voice ("Wij zijn...")
- Multi-vestiging: brand-essence hero, team deeper

## Category 2: Founder/team-photos & bios

### Edelman Trust Barometer — faces build trust
Visible faces drive trust formation:
- Authentic photos with natural expressions outperform stock
- Named individuals outperform anonymous "team"
- Founder visibility outperforms brand-anonymity
- Photo quality matters but authenticity matters more

### Cialdini liking principle
Visitors trust people they:
- Find physically and psychologically similar to themselves
- Find attractive (in the relaxed-friendly sense, not glamour)
- Have things in common with (hobbies, interests, values)

### Social-proof imagery research
- Eye-contact photos outperform side-profile by 20-30% in trust signals
- Smile (genuine Duchenne smile) outperforms neutral
- Authentic-context photos (in workplace, with tools) outperform studio
- Team-photo in natural-group beats individual-grid for warmth

### Service-business people-trust patterns
Specific to consumer-facing service:
- Photo + name + role minimum standard
- Adding hobby/interest mention (1-2 lines) lifts trust connection
- Years-of-experience visible
- Specialisation per team member
- Optional: signature/handwritten note from founder

### Best practices
- All team-members visible by photo + name + role
- Authentic photos (not stock-style identical-pose)
- Bios concise (50-100 words for klein team, 30-50 for groter team)
- Personal element per bio (hobby, interest, specialisatie)
- Founder bio fuller for solo or family-business
- Consistent photo-style across team (same photographer, similar lighting)

### Common failures
- Anonymous "team" page without names
- Stock-style photos (everyone same pose, same lighting, identical smile)
- Bios all-corporate ("X is passionate about Y") with no personality
- Founder hidden or absent
- Mixed photo-styles (some professional, some snapshots) within same team-grid
- Team-photo without names/labels

### Authenticity restraint
A finding may recommend verifying photo authenticity. A finding may NOT assert specific photos are stock without strong evidence (e.g. recognizable stock-photo composition, mismatch with brand-context, mismatch between team-size claim and unique people shown).

### Solo-vs-team calibration
- Solo: founder photo prominent, full bio with personal voice, optional family/lifestyle mentions where appropriate
- Klein team: each person equal weight, named, photographed, bio
- Groter team: leaders prominent, others in grid, departments structure
- Multi-vestiging: aggregate team-page + per-vestiging optional

## Category 3: Bedrijfsverhaal / oprichting / missie

### StoryBrand framework (Donald Miller)
Brand-story op aboutus moet customer-centered zijn:
- **Why this business exists** (in terms of customer pain solved)
- **Founder's expertise origin** (how they became qualified to solve this)
- **Approach difference** (what makes this brand's solution distinct)

### Narrative-trust research
- Specific stories outperform abstract values
- Vulnerability builds trust (founder admitting initial struggle)
- Time-anchoring strengthens story ("In 2014, after X happened...")
- Customer-pain-centered stories outperform brand-glorification

### Founder-story-impact research (Marketing Sherpa)
- B2C service businesses with founder-story have 20-40% higher engagement on aboutus
- Stories with specific dates, places, and motivations outperform vague "passion-driven" framings
- Stories with customer-impact metric (lives changed, problems solved) outperform inward-focused

### Best practices
- Concrete story with dates, places, motivations
- Customer-pain at center
- Founder vulnerability acceptable (initial challenge, lesson learned)
- Mission/values emerge FROM story, not stated abstractly
- Story length 200-400 words optimal (skim-friendly)
- Optional: photos illustrating story moments

### Common failures
- Abstract mission-vision-values walls
- "Our journey of excellence" type corporate-poetry
- No founder mentioned by name
- Story focuses on brand growth, not customer impact
- Generic founding date without context ("Established 2014")
- Vague values list ("Excellence · Passion · Quality")

### Business-type calibration
- Lokaal fysiek: lokale roots, community-connection
- Lokale service-provider: expertise-origin, why-this-niche
- Online-only: virtual-business-origin, founder accessibility
- Multi-vestiging: brand-genesis dominant, individual stories secondary

## Category 4: Expertise & credentials

### Cialdini authority
Expertise builds authority-trust. Specific credentials > vague claims:
- "BIG-geregistreerd (12345678)" beats "Erkend specialist"
- "15 jaar ervaring bij [erkende instelling]" beats "Jarenlange ervaring"
- "Gespecialiseerd in [niche]" beats "Allround expert"

### Edelman expertise-as-trust signal
B2C consumers verify expertise differently than B2B:
- Industry-relevant certifications (BIG voor zorg, NVM voor makelaars)
- Years operating in specific field
- Numerical evidence (klanten geholpen, projecten geleverd)
- Awards / recognitions concrete
- Continuing-education / specialisation deepening

### Best practices
- Credentials specific and verifiable
- Years experience visible per team member (for klein team)
- Specialisations named (not generic "expert")
- Awards/recognitions with year and source
- Continuing-education (recent courses, advanced specialisations)
- KvK / BTW / branche-keurmerken in trust-strip

### Common failures
- Generic "jarenlange ervaring" without specifics
- Award-walls with logos but no context
- Certifications-without-issuer
- Specialisaties als brede claims ("Expert in vele gebieden")
- Bullet-lists zonder narrative-context

### Business-type calibration
- Solo-provider: founder-credentials dominant, all visible
- Klein team: per-person credentials visible
- High-ticket service (juridisch, financieel): credentials very prominent
- Premium consumer service (luxe fotografie, premium salon): awards + recognitions
- Lokaal fysiek (kapsalon, restaurant): less formal credentials, more years-active + customer-count

## Category 5: Reviews & testimonials op aboutus (people-focused)

### Spiegel Research Center baseline
- Visible reviews lift conversion up to 270% (baseline)
- On aboutus specifically, effect is different: reviews here build PEOPLE-trust, not service-trust
- 4.2-4.7 rating optimum, not 5.0

### Named-testimonial-with-photo lift research
Specifically on aboutus:
- Named testimonials with photo outperform anonymous by 5-7x
- Person-focused testimonials ("Lisa knew exactly what I needed") outperform service-focused
- Testimonials mentioning specific team-members by name lift people-trust significantly
- Video testimonials high-leverage for warmth-driven brands

### Reviews-as-people-trust vs reviews-as-service-trust
On servicepage: "Het wortelkanaal verliep goed" (service-trust)
On aboutus: "Dr. Jansen nam de tijd om alles uit te leggen" (people-trust)

The latter directly supports the aboutus mission.

### Best practices
- 2-4 named testimonials with photos
- Person-focused content (mentioning team-member names)
- Specific context (situation, outcome, what specifically helped)
- Recent (within 1-2 years) where possible
- Optional: video testimonial
- Aboutus-specific testimonials, not duplicates from homepage

### Common failures
- Generic service-focused testimonials on aboutus
- Anonymous "Sarah M." quotes
- 5/5 perfect testimonials triggering skepticism
- Reuse of homepage testimonials without aboutus-specific context
- Testimonials older than 3 years undated

### Authenticity restraint
A finding may recommend verifying testimonial authenticity. A finding may NOT claim displayed testimonials are fabricated without strong evidence.

## Category 6: Use cases / impact / track record

### Cialdini social proof through narrative
Use cases on aboutus serve differently than on servicepage:
- On servicepage: "This service produces these results"
- On aboutus: "This is the kind of work we do, the impact we have"

The latter supports trust-building, not direct conversion.

### Concrete-evidence-over-claims research
Specific numbers > vague claims:
- "850+ bruiloften gefotografeerd" beats "veel ervaring"
- "12.000+ patiënten geholpen" beats "jarenlange klanten"
- "Sinds 2008 in deze buurt" beats "een lokale bekendheid"
- "30+ gemeenten waar wij actief zijn" beats "regionaal actief"

### Transformation-narrative research
- Before-after on outcome-driven services (esthetisch, makelaardij, klusbedrijven) builds trust
- Specific cases with dates and customers (where permission granted) > anonymized
- Time-anchored outcomes ("from problem in 3 weeks to result") build credibility

### Best practices
- Customer-count or project-count visible
- Years-active specific
- 2-3 case-study highlights (if relevant)
- Before-after for visual outcomes (where permission)
- Optional: locale-map with served-areas
- Optional: industry/sector breakdown

### Common failures
- Vague "many satisfied customers" claims
- No track-record numbers
- Case studies without permission documentation
- Generic stock before-after photos
- Outcomes without context (time, situation)

### Business-type calibration
- Solo-provider: project count + portfolio link
- Klein team: aggregate count + key cases
- Multi-vestiging: aggregate count + per-vestiging if relevant
- Online-only: virtual case studies + outcomes

## Category 7: Trust signals & credibility

### Edelman Trust Barometer — verifiable signals
B2C consumers need verifiable trust signals on aboutus:
- **Years operating** — "Sinds 2014" beats "Jarenlange ervaring"
- **Customer count** — specific number
- **KvK / BTW** — legal-presence verification (NL commercial sites)
- **Industry membership** — branche-organisatie lidmaatschap
- **Press / media mentions** — concrete sources
- **Awards / recognitions** — with year and issuer

### Aboutus-specific trust patterns
On aboutus, trust signals can be lighter than on homepage (visitor is already partly evaluating people, not just brand):
- Footer trust-strip (KvK, BTW, privacy) acceptable
- Inline trust signals woven into bio/story acceptable
- Optional dedicated "Erkenningen" strip
- AVG-compliance microcopy if any form on aboutus

### Best practices
- Trust signals woven into narrative (not bolted-on banner)
- Specific over vague
- Recent verifications (recent awards beat old awards)
- Industry-relevant only (no generic logos)
- Footer-strip with legal info

### Common failures
- Generic certification logos without context
- "Jarenlange ervaring" without years
- Award-walls disconnected from narrative
- KvK / privacy ontbreekt (NL commercial sites)
- Self-claims without third-party validation

## Category 8: Visuele authenticiteit

### Authenticity-over-polish research (Marketing Sherpa)
Especially for warmth-driven brands:
- Stock photos on aboutus actively damage trust
- Authentic team/operation imagery builds connection
- Behind-the-scenes content lifts engagement 30-50%
- Imperfect-real beats perfect-stock

### Stock-photo trust penalty
Recognizable stock-photo composition signals "not real":
- Identical-pose team photos
- Generic "office life" shots
- Smiling diverse-team in branded-clothes
- Sterile photo-studio backgrounds
- Hands-on-keyboard close-ups

### Behind-the-scenes content patterns
High-leverage authentic content:
- Founder/team in workplace doing actual work
- Office/practice photos showing real environment
- Tools-of-trade imagery (specific to business-type)
- Optional: short founder-video introduction
- Optional: time-lapse of typical workday

### Best practices
- All photos are authentic (real team, real workplace)
- Consistent photographer / photo-style across page
- Behind-the-scenes content visible
- Workplace/practice/office photos
- Optional: founder-introduction video (30-60 seconds)
- Mobile-optimized image variants

### Common failures
- Stock team photos
- Generic "office" stock imagery
- Mixed photo styles (some pro, some snapshot, no coherence)
- No workplace photos
- Heavy stock-photo background in founder portrait

### Authenticity restraint
A finding may recommend verifying imagery authenticity. A finding may NOT assert specific photos are stock without strong evidence (e.g. recognizable stock-photo composition pattern, recognizable model from common stock libraries).

### Calibration
- Premium brands: more polish acceptable, but should still feel authentic
- Volume / value brands: warmth + authentic dominate
- Family-business / boutique: full authentic
- High-ticket professional (advocaat, financieel): professional but not stock

## Category 9: Persoonlijke benadering & waarden

### Values-as-differentiator research
For B2C service-business, values often determine selection between qualified providers:
- Warm vs zakelijk (warm vs business-like)
- Gestructureerd vs flexibel (structured vs flexible)
- Premium vs toegankelijk (premium vs accessible)
- Educatief vs uitvoerend (educational vs executive)
- Traditioneel vs innovatief (traditional vs innovative)

Two qualified tandartsen with different styles: consumer chooses based on values-fit, not just credentials.

### Warmth-competence balance (Fiske/Cuddy)
Aboutus must communicate BOTH:
- Warmth signals (smiles, casual moments, hobbies, accessibility)
- Competence signals (credentials, years, specialisations, outcomes)

Pure warmth = "nice but probably amateur"
Pure competence = "skilled but probably cold"

### Personality-fit research in service-business
Consumer's primary unspoken question: "Would I feel comfortable working with these people?"
- Answer comes from communication style (formal vs casual)
- Visual cues (clothing, posture, smile-style)
- Stated values matched with shown behavior

### Best practices
- Values stated CONCRETELY (with examples of how they show up in service)
- Personal touches in bios (hobbies, family-mentions if appropriate)
- Tone matches values (warm brand = warm copy; professional brand = professional copy)
- "How we work" section showing values in practice
- Optional: founder-philosophy quote

### Common failures
- Generic values walls ("Quality · Trust · Excellence")
- Tone mismatch (warm brand with corporate copy)
- Values without examples
- Stated values mismatched with visible team-personality
- Corporate-jargon in warmth-driven brand

### Business-type calibration
- Warmth-driven (kapsalon, fotografie, restaurant): values prominent, personal touches dominant
- Professional (advocaat, financieel adviseur): values structured, competence-balanced
- Lokaal fysiek with community-feel (buurt kapsalon, lokaal restaurant): community-values prominent
- Multi-vestiging: values aggregate at brand-level

## Category 10: CTA-routing naar conversie

### Soft-CTA patterns on aboutus
Aboutus is NOT a primary conversion page. CTAs should be:
- Soft (not aggressive)
- Contextual (woven into content)
- Multi-path (route to different next-pages based on visitor interest)
- Lower-friction than service/dedicated LP CTAs

### Aboutus-as-trust-routing
The aboutus job is to BUILD trust, then route to conversion pages:
- "Wil je weten welke diensten we aanbieden? → [Service-page]"
- "Klaar voor een gesprek? → [Contact-page]"
- "Benieuwd hoe wij werken? → [Aanpak-page]"
- Founder/team mentions can include "Ontmoet [Naam] persoonlijk → [Contact]"

### Contextual CTAs in narrative pages
- End of founder-story: "Plan een kennismakingsgesprek met [Naam]"
- End of credentials: "Bekijk onze diensten"
- End of values-section: "Lees hoe wij werken"
- After testimonials: "Plan jouw afspraak"

### Best practices
- Multiple soft CTAs throughout page (not just bottom)
- Contextual to surrounding content
- Action-oriented but not aggressive
- Route to relevant next-page (not generic "Contact")
- Optional: sticky-mobile CTA matched to primary conversion path
- Footer-CTA as safety net

### Common failures
- No CTAs on aboutus (visitor reaches end with no next-step)
- Single aggressive CTA at end (mismatch with trust-building atmosphere)
- Generic "Contact us" at footer only
- Multiple competing aggressive CTAs (anti-trust pattern)
- CTAs disconnected from surrounding content

### Business-type calibration
- Solo-provider: founder-direct CTAs ("Bel direct met [Naam]")
- Klein team: per-person CTAs where relevant
- Online-only: form / video-consultation CTAs
- Multi-vestiging: vestiging-zoeker as routing

## Category 11: Mobile experience

### Baymard mobile commerce research applied to aboutus
- Mobile dominates aboutus traffic for B2C service (65-80%)
- Mobile aboutus engagement (time-on-page, scroll-depth) lags desktop
- Sticky mobile CTA on aboutus is debatable (trust-building vs aggressive routing)

### Mobile aboutus-page patterns
- Team-grid mobile-optimized (single column, large photos)
- Bios collapsible on mobile (read-more pattern)
- Founder-video mobile-friendly (no autoplay, low data)
- Mobile-first navigation
- Footer sticky-light (logo + key contact)

### Best practices
- Single-column layout on mobile
- Photos crisp and tap-friendly
- Bios collapsible if long
- Video lightweight on mobile (or replace with hero-image)
- Scrolling smooth, no jumps
- Fast page load

### Common failures
- Desktop team-grid ported to mobile (multi-column too narrow)
- Bios full-text on mobile (overwhelming)
- Heavy hero-video that slows mobile load
- Sticky CTAs blocking content
- Photos too small to recognize faces on mobile

## Brand calibration notes

Always recalibrate by brand snapshot AND business-type AND solo-vs-team-structure:

**Example: Hero**
- Solo kapper: founder photo + first-person hero ("Ik ben [Naam], ik knip al 15 jaar...")
- Klein tandartsteam: team-photo + team-voice hero ("Wij zijn een team van 4 tandartsen...")
- Multi-vestiging keten: brand-essence hero, team deeper ("Sinds 1998 zorg in 14 vestigingen...")
- Online financieel adviseur: founder-video + introduction hero

**Example: Team-photos emphasis**
- Lokaal fysiek (kapsalon): team-photos kritiek, warmth-driven
- Online-only: founder-photo + team-credentials (compensation voor geen fysieke locatie)
- High-ticket professional: professional team-photos met credentials prominent

**Example: Storytelling depth**
- Solo-provider: deep founder-story (200-400 woorden)
- Klein team: collective story + individual brief bios
- Multi-vestiging: brand-story aggregate, individual stories optional

Recommendations that don't match brand AND business-type AND structure zijn fout.
