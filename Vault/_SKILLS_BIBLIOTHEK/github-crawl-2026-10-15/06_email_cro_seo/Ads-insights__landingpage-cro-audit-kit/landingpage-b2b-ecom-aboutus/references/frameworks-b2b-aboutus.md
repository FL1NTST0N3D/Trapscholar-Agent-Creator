# Frameworks for B2B Ecommerce About Us Page Audits

This reference file contains the CRO and B2B-specific frameworks, principles, and applied research used in the B2B about us page audit. Each section maps to a finding category from the main skill.

---

## CRITICAL — Visual verification before every finding

B2B about us trust work depends heavily on visual authenticity:
- Real office/facility photography vs stock imagery
- Authentic leadership/team photography
- Genuine customer logo wall vs decorative imagery
- Certification logos with year/number visibility
- Case-study imagery
- Mobile rendering of long-form content

**For every finding, verify the claim against screenshots before delivering.** A finding that says "no team visible" while the screenshot shows it is worse than no finding.

---

## CRITICAL — Authenticity claims require restraint (B2B-specific risks)

B2B about us pages often look corporate by necessity — formal team photos, glossy office imagery, polished client testimonials. **Do not label these "stock" or "fake" without strong evidence.**

**The hard rules:**
- A finding may RECOMMEND verifying authenticity and considering alternatives
- A finding may NOT assert specific photos are stock
- A finding may NOT assert specific testimonials are fabricated
- A finding may NOT assert specific certifications are invalid or fake
- A finding may NOT assert specific customer logos are unauthorized

When in doubt: "Consider verifying [X] and adding signals of authenticity (year, certificate number, customer-permission documentation)" rather than "Your [X] is fake."

This protects the user from acting on false accusations and protects audit credibility.

---

## CRITICAL — B2B type-specific calibration

The four B2B types require fundamentally different about-us recommendations:

**Transactional B2B ecom:**
- Focus on capability, scale, reliability
- Customer count, order volume, fulfillment speed
- Repeat-order positioning
- Self-service-friendly tone

**Quote-driven B2B:**
- Focus on engineering expertise, project capability
- Project examples and case studies
- Sales-team accessibility prominent
- Authority signals (engineers' credentials, technical depth)

**Wholesale (trade):**
- Focus on supply chain depth, brand portfolio
- Distribution capability, multi-region presence
- Trade-program benefits framed prominently
- Reseller-relationship positioning

**Hybrid:**
- Mixed positioning, often layered

Recommendations for one type are usually wrong for another.

## Core frameworks (apply across the entire audit)

### Edelman B2B Trust Barometer 2024-2025
Most relevant framework for B2B about us:
- **73% of B2B buyers say trust in the supplier is critical to closing** (vs 45% for B2C)
- Verifiable claims outperform vague claims by **4-6x** in B2B trust impact (even more than B2C)
- Independent third-party signals (certifications, audited specs, customer logos with verification) dominate
- Multi-stakeholder trust: end-users want capability, procurement wants stability, finance wants longevity, IT wants compliance
- B2B about us must serve all these audiences simultaneously

### April Dunford positioning
"Obviously Awesome" framework critical for B2B about us:
- **Category context** — what is this company, exactly? (Many B2B buyers don't know your category by your brand's term)
- **Differentiator clarity** — why this supplier vs competitors the buyer is considering?
- **"For who" specificity** — which buyer types is this designed for?
- **Trends and forces** — why this category, why now?

B2B about us pages that fail Dunford's framework leave buyers confused about whether the company is even relevant.

### Challenger Sale (Dixon & Adamson)
B2B buyers respond to suppliers who teach, tailor, and take control:
- About us as "teach": educational framing of the industry problem
- About us as "tailor": speak to specific buyer roles (engineer, procurement, etc.)
- About us as "control": confident, opinionated positioning vs commodity alternatives

### MEDDIC framework on B2B about us
- **Metrics:** about us should reference business outcomes ("Our customers reduce procurement costs by 23%")
- **Economic buyer:** about us serves financial decision-maker via stability + scale signals
- **Decision criteria:** about us proves capability matches what buyers compare
- **Decision process:** about us aids champion's internal advocacy (shareable, downloadable, credible)
- **Identify pain:** about us shows understanding of customer pain
- **Champion enablement:** about us gives the champion proof artifacts (case studies, white papers, references)

### Baymard B2B research on about us
B2B about us is less directly tested than PDP or checkout, but Baymard's principles apply:
- B2B buyers visit about us during multi-stakeholder evaluation (often weeks before purchase)
- About us influences shortlist inclusion (am I evaluating this supplier?)
- About us is one of the highest-engagement pages for procurement-led evaluation

### Cialdini's principles on B2B about us
- **Authority** — credentials, certifications, customer logos, press, awards
- **Social proof** — customer count, named-customer testimonials, industry-specific endorsements
- **Liking** — leadership accessibility, "we share your industry context"
- **Commitment-consistency** — published values create accountability
- **Reciprocity** — shared expertise (white papers, industry reports) before asking for purchase
- **Scarcity** — limited capacity, specialized expertise, exclusive distribution

### Nielsen Norman B2B about us research
- B2B about us pages get 2-5% of total site traffic (similar to B2C)
- Visitors who view about us are 2-4x more likely to convert downstream (selection + influence effect)
- Scan in F-pattern; ~80% scan rather than read
- Multi-session: B2B visitors often return to about us multiple times across a buying cycle

### MECLABS Conversion Sequence Heuristic on B2B about us
About us reduces **anxiety** primarily:
- Supplier viability anxiety: "Will this company still exist in 3 years?"
- Compliance anxiety: "Can I procure from this supplier under our policies?"
- Quality anxiety: "Do they have the operational discipline for our standards?"
- Escalation anxiety: "If something goes wrong, who do I talk to?"

### Jobs-to-be-Done on B2B about us
B2B visitors on about us are doing one of:
1. **Supplier viability check** — most common; financial stability + longevity
2. **Compliance verification** — certifications, standards, certifications visible
3. **Capability evaluation** — capacity, scale, technical depth
4. **Champion enablement** — finding shareable proof for internal advocacy
5. **Personnel research** — who would I work with? (account managers, technical contacts)

Each job has different needs. A well-designed B2B about us serves the dominant jobs.

**Important Dutch translation:** never use "huren" for JTBD. Use "kiezen voor", "zoeken om vertrouwen op te bouwen", "kopers die bevestiging zoeken".

## Category 1: Above-the-fold positioning & supplier promise

### April Dunford on B2B opening
The above-the-fold must answer: **"What is this company and why should I evaluate it as a supplier?"** within 5 seconds.

Bad: "Welcome to [Brand], a leading provider of quality solutions."
Good: "[Brand] supplies [specific category] to [specific industry] across [region]. [Differentiator]."

### Challenger Sale on B2B opening
Teach + tailor:
- Identify the buyer's industry context immediately
- Position the brand's specific expertise relative to that context
- Avoid generic "leading provider" language

### Best practices
- Headline names the category and target customer
- Subheadline differentiates from category alternatives
- One sentence of credibility anchor (years, customer count, scale)
- Visual: authentic facility/operations imagery, not stock

### Common failures
- "We are a leading [generic]" — says nothing concrete
- Category context missing (visitors don't know what category to evaluate against)
- Founded-in-year-X opening without customer relevance
- Stock imagery contradicting later authentic photos

### B2B type calibration
- Transactional: scale + reliability statement
- Quote-driven: expertise + capability statement
- Wholesale: brand portfolio + distribution capability statement

## Category 2: Company story, mission & operating principles

### Challenger Sale teach + tailor structure
Effective B2B company story follows:
1. **The category problem we saw** (teach)
2. **What we built differently** (tailor)
3. **The operating principles that guide our work** (values made concrete)
4. **The impact we've delivered to customers** (proof)

### April Dunford on B2B story
The story should make procurement, end-users, AND finance all feel: "These people understand our context."

### Best practices
- Opening with industry problem, not company history
- Concrete details (specific founding insight, specific first major customer)
- Operating principles concrete, not generic ("Quality" — bad; "We test every batch against ISO 9001 standards and publish quality reports quarterly" — good)
- Length: 250-500 words for the main story

### Common failures
- Pure chronological timeline that buries customer relevance
- Aspirational language without evidence
- Generic founding story
- Self-positioning as hero rather than guide
- Story-as-longer-homepage-pitch

## Category 3: Leadership, expertise & team credibility

### Cialdini authority on B2B
B2B visitors trust visible expertise:
- Named leaders with relevant credentials
- Engineers/technical leads visible (for engineering-driven brands)
- Industry tenure (years in category)
- Published thought leadership (white papers, industry talks)
- Educational backgrounds when relevant (engineering credentials for technical products)

### MEDDIC Champion enablement
The B2B Champion needs identifiable people to advocate to internally:
- "Our team includes [Senior Engineer Name] — formerly at [respected industry name]"
- LinkedIn-linked team members (procurement can verify backgrounds)
- Speaker credentials at industry conferences
- Patent holdings or technical contributions

### Edelman B2B Trust Barometer
Leadership visibility correlates with supplier trust in B2B contexts:
- Visible CEO/founder with industry background outperforms anonymous corporate
- Engineering/technical leadership visible alongside executive leadership
- Cross-functional team (engineering + customer success + operations) builds completeness

### Best practices
- Leadership section with photos, names, roles, brief backgrounds
- Industry credentials specific (years, prior roles, certifications)
- LinkedIn-linked profiles where possible
- Mix of executive + technical leadership
- For small/founder-led brands: founder prominence appropriate
- For corporate B2B: leadership only (not full team), more formal presentation

### Common failures
- "Our team" as headline with no actual team shown
- Stock photos of business people
- Leadership without industry context (just titles)
- No technical/engineering leadership for technical products
- Founder-only when company is mid-sized (signals key-person-risk)

### B2B type calibration
- Transactional: standard leadership + customer success focus
- Quote-driven: engineering/technical leadership prominent
- Wholesale: sales/distribution leadership prominent
- All: visible accessibility (contact paths to leadership when appropriate)

## Category 4: Scale, longevity & operational footprint

### Edelman B2B Trust — operational signals
B2B buyers need to verify supplier viability before committing:
- **Years operating** — "Established 1987" beats "Years of experience"
- **Customer count** — "Trusted by 1,800+ businesses across 23 countries"
- **Order volume** — "We process 50,000+ orders per year"
- **Geographic footprint** — offices, warehouses, support regions
- **Employee count** — broad indicator of scale
- **Revenue indication** — for public companies or when willing to share
- **Industry tenure** — years in this specific vertical

### Cialdini authority + social proof stacked
Scale claims work hardest when verifiable:
- Specific numbers ("1,847 active customers" beats "thousands")
- Dated claims ("Since 1987 — 37 years in industrial supply")
- Geographic specificity ("Offices in Amsterdam, Berlin, Madrid")
- Verifiable scale ("ISO-audited capacity: 12,000 units/month")

### Best practices
- Scale signals visible above the fold or in dedicated section
- Specific numbers always over vague claims
- Map of operational locations if multi-region
- Year-founded visible
- Customer/order count with optional growth trend
- Facility imagery (warehouse, production line, offices) for operational credibility

### Common failures
- "Years of experience" without specifying
- "Thousands of customers" — unverifiable
- No geographic footprint despite multi-country claim
- Customer/order count rounded suspiciously ("over 1,000+")
- No visible facilities despite manufacturing claims

### B2B type calibration
- Transactional: order volume + customer count + fulfillment-speed metrics
- Quote-driven: project count + multi-region capability + engineering capacity
- Wholesale: distribution capacity + brand portfolio + warehouse footprint

## Category 5: Certifications, compliance & industry standards

### Cialdini authority via third-party verification
B2B buyers operate in regulated environments. Certifications are:
- Quality: ISO 9001, ISO 14001, Six Sigma
- Industry-specific: HACCP (food), ISO 13485 (medical), ISO/TS 16949 (automotive)
- Compliance: CE, FDA, REACH, RoHS
- Environmental: B-Corp, FSC, Cradle to Cradle
- Trade: AEO (customs), Thuiswinkel Waarborg

Missing required certifications on regulated category is a deal-breaker — procurement cannot specify non-compliant suppliers.

### Edelman B2B Trust — verifiable signals
- Logos visible
- Certification numbers + dates
- Downloadable certificates
- Issuing-body links

### Best practices
- Certifications visible in dedicated section
- Logos accompanied by year + certificate number
- Certificate downloads (PDF) for verification
- Industry-specific certifications grouped by category
- Validity dates (expiry) shown for time-sensitive certifications

### Common failures
- Logos without dates (looks dated or fake)
- Generic "ISO certified" without specifying which ISO
- Certificates not downloadable (visitor cannot verify)
- Out-of-date certifications still displayed
- Missing required certifications for regulated category
- Decorative use of certification logos in marketing copy

## Category 6: Customer references, logos & case studies

### Cialdini social proof in B2B
- **Customer logos** — recognizable brands signal credibility
- **Named customer testimonials** — with role, company, specific outcome
- **Case studies** — depth proof for evaluation
- **Industry-specific customers** — visitors look for "do they work with companies like mine?"
- **Customer count** — specific over vague

### MEDDIC Champion enablement
B2B champions need shareable proof:
- Downloadable case studies (PDF)
- Customer-logo walls for visual credibility
- Reference-customer program (vetted references available on request)
- Specific outcome metrics ("Reduced procurement cycle time 31% for [Company]")

### Spiegel Research Center
Named, photo-attributed, role-specified testimonials outperform anonymous quotes by 5-7x in trust impact — same finding holds in B2B with even stronger effect.

### Best practices
- Customer-logo wall with 8-15 recognizable brands (visitors scan for familiar names)
- 2-3 case studies with: customer name, industry, problem, solution, outcome with metrics
- Customer testimonials with full attribution: name, role, company, photo
- Industry diversity (don't show only one vertical)
- Recent references (within 1-2 years)

### Common failures
- Logo wall with unrecognizable or stock-looking brands
- Anonymous "Sarah M." testimonials
- Case studies without outcome metrics
- Single-industry references (suggests narrow capability)
- Old references (3+ years undated)
- No downloadable case studies

### Permission and authenticity restraint
A finding may recommend documenting permission-to-use for customer logos. A finding may NOT assert that displayed logos are unauthorized. If suspicious, recommend verification rather than accusation.

## Category 7: Supply chain, manufacturing & sourcing transparency

### Edelman B2B Trust — supply chain transparency
Increasingly important for B2B trust:
- Where products are sourced
- Manufacturing locations (own facilities vs outsourced)
- Supplier audit programs
- Conflict mineral / fair labor practices (for relevant categories)
- Sustainability of supply chain

### April Dunford — proof through specificity
Vague supply chain claims fail. Specific claims build trust:
- "Sourced from 14 certified suppliers across EU and Japan" beats "Global supply chain"
- "Manufactured in our Eindhoven facility, ISO 9001 audited" beats "Quality manufacturing"
- "100% traceability via batch numbers" beats "Quality control"

### Best practices
- Supply chain section visible
- Specific manufacturing locations named
- Audit/certification of suppliers
- Traceability commitments specific
- For sustainability-conscious categories: detailed sustainability claims

### Common failures
- Generic "global supply chain"
- Vague "quality manufacturers"
- No traceability commitment for categories where it matters
- Sustainability claims without evidence
- Missing supply chain content for industries where it's expected (food, fashion, electronics)

## Category 8: Visual storytelling

### Nielsen Norman visual-first on B2B
B2B about us photography requirements:
- Authentic office/facility imagery
- Operations in progress (warehouse, manufacturing, fulfillment)
- Team at work (vs staged portraits)
- Products in industry context (installed, in use)
- Quality of imagery matches brand maturity

### Edelman trust on B2B photography
- Authentic operational imagery outperforms glossy corporate stock
- Industry-specific contexts (factories, project sites, warehouses) outperform generic office shots
- Mobile-quality photos can hurt premium B2B; corporate-quality photos can hurt small specialty

### Best practices
- Mix: facility shots, operations shots, team-at-work, products-in-context
- Consistent visual treatment
- Authentic over stock
- Resolution adequate for retina displays
- Video where appropriate (factory tour, founder message, customer testimonial)

### Common failures
- All stock photography
- Mixed authentic + stock (creates dissonance)
- No operational imagery despite manufacturing claims
- Decorative photos without content
- Stock business-people imagery

## Category 9: Sustainability / ESG / corporate responsibility (where applicable)

**Brand-conditional category. If brand makes no ESG claims AND category is not impact-driven, mark as "not applicable". Do NOT invent recommendations.**

### Edelman B2B Trust — ESG findings
- Increasingly important in B2B procurement (especially for public/large customers)
- Specific verifiable claims outperform general statements by 4-5x
- Third-party certifications (B-Corp, Science-Based Targets, FSC) outperform self-claims
- Procurement evaluation increasingly weights ESG criteria

### Best practices (when applicable)
- Specific verifiable claims with metrics
- Third-party certifications visible
- Annual ESG/CSR reports linked
- Honest acknowledgment of progress and challenges
- Supply chain transparency

### When NOT to recommend ESG content
- Brand makes no claims
- Category is impact-neutral or sustainability-irrelevant
- Brand voice is fundamentally about other things
- Adding ESG would feel forced

## Category 10: Contact, approachability & escalation paths

### MECLABS anxiety axis
B2B contact accessibility reduces escalation anxiety:
- "Who do I call when something goes wrong?"
- "Is there a dedicated account manager?"
- "How fast is response time?"

### Cialdini liking via accessibility
Visible accessibility signals make the company feel like a partner, not a vendor:
- Account-manager assignments visible
- Phone, email, address prominent
- Response-time commitments
- Office hours
- Multi-language support

### Best practices
- Phone number visible (not hidden behind contact form)
- Office addresses for all locations
- Customer service hours
- Account manager assignment for active customers
- Email with named contact (or department)
- Multi-language support indicators
- Showroom/visit information for relevant verticals

### Common failures
- Contact only via web form
- No phone number anywhere
- "Contact us" link without channel visibility
- Hours not specified
- Anonymous "info@" emails
- No escalation path for service issues

### B2B type calibration
- Transactional: customer-service-stack prominent
- Quote-driven: sales-rep contact + technical-support contact
- Wholesale: account-manager contact + trade-program contact
- All: physical location signals operational legitimacy

## Category 11: Visual hierarchy, scanability & cross-links to conversion paths

### Nielsen Norman F-pattern on B2B about us
~80% of about us visitors scan rather than read. Critical information must be in headlines, subheads, and first sentences. Walls of text without scanning aids lose readers.

### WiderFunnel LIFT clarity factor
About us must not be a dead-end. Trust-built visitors need clear next steps:
- "View our product catalog"
- "Request a quote"
- "Speak to a specialist"
- "Download our capabilities brochure"

### Best practices
- H2 subheadings for each major section
- Short paragraphs (3-5 sentences max)
- Image breaks every 200-400 words
- Bulleted lists for capabilities, certifications, milestones
- Pull quotes for key claims
- Closing CTAs aligned to B2B type

### Common failures
- Walls of unbroken text
- No section subheadings
- Dead-end at page bottom (no CTAs)
- Generic "Contact us" CTA without value framing
- Aggressive sales CTAs after a trust-building story
- Multi-column body text breaking F-pattern reading

### B2B-specific CTAs
- Transactional: "View product catalog" + "Open trade account"
- Quote-driven: "Request a quote" + "Schedule a consultation"
- Wholesale: "Apply for trade pricing" + "Download wholesale catalog"

## Brand calibration notes

Always recalibrate by brand snapshot AND B2B type:

**Example: Leadership presentation**
- Small specialist B2B: founder + key engineers prominent, warm context
- Mid-sized B2B: leadership team + departmental leads
- Corporate B2B: executive team only, formal presentation
- Wholesale: distribution/sales leadership + brand portfolio

**Example: Story emphasis**
- Transactional: capability, scale, reliability
- Quote-driven: expertise, engineering capability, project examples
- Wholesale: supply chain depth, brand portfolio, distribution capability

**Example: Closing CTA**
- Transactional: "View catalog" + "Open trade account"
- Quote-driven: "Request a quote" + "Speak to a specialist"
- Wholesale: "Apply for trade pricing" + "Download wholesale catalog"

Recommendations that don't match brand AND B2B type are wrong.

## Notes on confidence calibration for B2B about us

B2B about us has even less public A/B-test data than B2C about us. Calibrate ICE Confidence:

- **Confidence 8-9:** when recommendation is directly supported by Baymard or Edelman B2B with explicit evidence (rare)
- **Confidence 6-7:** when recommendation follows sound B2B trust-building principles
- **Confidence 4-5:** when recommendation is principle-grounded but lacks direct test evidence
- **Confidence 2-3:** when recommendation requires research first

This is honest, not weak.

## Test-readiness check

Before finalizing each finding:
- [ ] Diagnosis cites a specific principle
- [ ] Recommendation is concrete
- [ ] Hypothesis follows "If X, then Y, because Z"
- [ ] Primary metric is named (B2B about-us metrics: exit rate, click-through to contact-quote-product, multi-session conversion, account-creation)
- [ ] ICE justified by I/C/E breakdown
- [ ] Recommendation matches brand AND B2B type
- [ ] No authenticity-state claims
- [ ] No certification-validity claims
- [ ] Dutch output: no "huren" check
- [ ] ESG recommendation only if brand has genuine claims

If any unchecked, rework before delivering.
