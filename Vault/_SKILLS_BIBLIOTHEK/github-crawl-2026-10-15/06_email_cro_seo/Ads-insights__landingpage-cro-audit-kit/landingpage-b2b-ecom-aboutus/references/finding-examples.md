# Worked finding examples — B2B about us page quality calibration

These examples show what a high-quality B2B about us finding looks like across different B2B types, brand snapshots, and ICE scores. Use them as a reference standard.

---

## Example 1: 🔴 Critical — Above-the-fold positioning & supplier promise (quote-driven engineering supplier)

### 🔴 Above-the-fold — "Leading provider" headline says nothing about category or buyer

**Diagnosis**
The page opens with "We are a leading provider of quality engineering solutions" — a sentence that fails April Dunford's three positioning tests simultaneously: no category context (engineering solutions could mean software, hardware, civil, mechanical, hydraulic, anything), no differentiator (every supplier in every category claims "leading" and "quality"), and no "for who" specificity (who is this supposed to help). Per Edelman B2B Trust Barometer, **verifiable specific claims outperform vague claims by 4-6x** in B2B trust impact — and the current headline is the platonic example of an unverifiable vague claim. A procurement specialist or specifier landing here from a sourcing exercise has no information to determine whether this supplier is worth evaluating, and the typical response is back-button within 8 seconds.

**Recommendation**
Replace the opening with a specific category + customer + differentiator structure. Concrete pattern: "[Brand] engineers custom [specific product category] for [specific industry] across [region]. [Differentiator anchored in proof]." Example replacement: "Bakker Industrial engineers custom hydraulic systems for offshore wind and heavy lift across Northern Europe. ISO 9001 audited; 47 active multi-year contracts." The subheadline can carry one credibility anchor: "Since 1987 · 230 employees · Eindhoven & Aberdeen offices." Pair with authentic operational imagery (a hydraulic system being assembled or installed) rather than stock business-people imagery. The goal is for a procurement specialist to know within 5 seconds whether this supplier matches their sourcing need.

**Test specification**
- **Hypothesis:** If we replace the generic "leading provider" headline with a specific category + customer + differentiator structure, then about-us exit rate will decrease and click-through to product catalog / quote request will increase because procurement-led visitors immediately understand whether to evaluate this supplier (April Dunford positioning; Edelman B2B Trust Barometer).
- **Variant A:** "We are a leading provider of quality engineering solutions"
- **Variant B:** Category + industry + region + differentiator headline with credibility anchor subhead
- **Primary metric:** about-us click-through rate to product catalog or quote-request CTA
- **Secondary metrics:** about-us exit rate, scroll depth, multi-session return rate
- **Expected impact:** +15% to +35% on about-us click-through rate (relative gains on small absolute volume)
- **ICE:** I=7, C=8, E=10 → 8.3
- **Source:** April Dunford "Obviously Awesome" positioning; Edelman B2B Trust Barometer 2024-2025

## Example 2: 🔴 Critical — Certifications, compliance & industry standards (regulated category)

### 🔴 Certifications — Logos shown without dates, numbers, or downloadable certificates

**Diagnosis**
The page displays five certification logos in a row (ISO 9001, ISO 14001, CE, FSC, B-Corp) but provides no certificate numbers, no issue/expiry dates, no issuing-body links, and no downloadable certificates. Per Edelman B2B Trust Barometer, **verifiable third-party signals dominate B2B trust-building** — and verification fails when the visitor cannot confirm what they're being shown is real. Per Cialdini's authority principle, authority works through verifiability, not visual reference. For a regulated B2B category (food packaging supplier serving HACCP-bound customers), unverified certification logos are worse than no logos: procurement teams who routinely verify supplier certifications against issuing-body databases will treat the page as not-yet-proven, not as proven.

**Recommendation**
For each certification logo, add four verification elements directly adjacent:
1. Certificate number (e.g., "ISO 9001:2015 — Cert No. NL-2018-12847")
2. Issue and expiry dates ("Valid 12-2023 until 12-2026")
3. Issuing body name with link ("Issued by Lloyd's Register")
4. Downloadable PDF certificate (one-click access)

Group certifications by category in a dedicated section: Quality (ISO 9001), Environmental (ISO 14001), Product compliance (CE), Material sourcing (FSC), Corporate responsibility (B-Corp). Use a small grid layout with each certification's verification block beneath the logo. For certifications likely to be unfamiliar to some visitors, add a one-line explanation ("FSC: forest stewardship certification covering raw material sourcing"). Avoid using certification logos decoratively elsewhere on the site — concentrate them in the dedicated section to preserve their authority weight.

**Test specification**
- **Hypothesis:** If we add certificate numbers, dates, issuing bodies, and downloadable PDFs to each certification logo, then supplier-trust signals to procurement-evaluating visitors will increase, measured downstream as account-creation rate and first-order conversion from about-us referrer (Edelman B2B Trust Barometer; Cialdini authority through verifiability).
- **Variant A:** logos only, no verification details
- **Variant B:** logos with certificate numbers, dates, issuing body, downloadable PDF
- **Primary metric:** about-us-referred account-creation rate (over 30-90 day window due to longer cycle)
- **Secondary metrics:** certificate-download rate, time-on-page in certification section, multi-session return rate
- **Expected impact:** +5% to +15% on about-us-referred conversion (cumulative across longer sales cycle)
- **ICE:** I=7, C=8, E=7 → 7.3
- **Source:** Edelman B2B Trust Barometer 2024-2025; Cialdini "Influence" (authority principle)

## Example 3: 🔴 Critical — Customer references, logos & case studies (wholesale fashion B2B)

### 🔴 Customer references — Logo wall absent; no named-customer testimonials; no case studies

**Diagnosis**
The about-us page contains zero customer references: no logo wall, no named-customer testimonials, no case studies, no industry-specific endorsements. For a fashion-wholesale platform serving boutique retailers, this is the largest structural trust gap on the page. Per Cialdini's social proof principle, B2B buyers in evaluation mode look specifically for "do they work with companies like mine?" — and the absence of recognizable customer signals leaves that question unanswered. Per Spiegel Research Center, named-photo-attributed testimonials outperform anonymous quotes by 5-7x in trust impact, and the effect is stronger in B2B because procurement teams want verifiable references. Per MEDDIC's Champion enablement principle, the B2B champion needs shareable proof for internal advocacy — and a champion exploring this supplier has nothing to send to procurement.

**Recommendation**
Three additions, in priority order:
1. **Customer logo wall**: 12-18 recognizable boutique-retailer logos, grouped by region (NL, BE, DE, FR) to signal geographic capability. Logos must be confirmed authorized (recommend documenting permission via standard customer-reference agreements). Avoid stock-looking decorative logos.
2. **3-5 named-customer testimonials** with photo, name, role, company, and specific outcome metric. Pattern: "Working with [Brand] reduced our restocking lead time from 12 weeks to 6 — and the trade pricing tier system lets me forecast margins precisely." — Marieke van Dijk, Owner, Atelier Roest Amsterdam.
3. **2-3 downloadable case studies** (PDF, 2-4 pages each) covering: problem the boutique faced, solution implemented, outcome with metrics. Make these accessible without form-gating (full lead capture in case-study material itself is acceptable; gating the download adds friction at the wrong moment).

Implementation requires customer-reference program — recommend dedicating customer-success time to securing 6-10 confirmed references before launch.

**Test specification**
- **Hypothesis:** If we add a customer logo wall, named testimonials, and downloadable case studies, then about-us-referred account-applications will increase substantially because B2B champions gain the shareable proof they need to advocate internally to procurement (Cialdini social proof; MEDDIC Champion enablement; Spiegel testimonial-attribution research).
- **Variant A:** no customer references anywhere on about-us page
- **Variant B:** logo wall + 3-5 named testimonials + 2-3 downloadable case studies
- **Primary metric:** about-us-referred trade-account applications (over 60-90 day window)
- **Secondary metrics:** case-study download rate, multi-session return rate, time-on-page
- **Expected impact:** +20% to +50% on about-us-referred trade-account applications (large because baseline is structurally suppressed)
- **ICE:** I=8, C=8, E=5 → 7.0
- **Source:** Cialdini social proof; Spiegel Research Center testimonial-attribution research; MEDDIC framework (Champion enablement)

## Example 4: 🟠 Important — Scale, longevity & operational footprint (mid-sized industrial supplier)

### 🟠 Scale & longevity — Vague "decades of experience" claim with no specific anchor

**Diagnosis**
The page states "With decades of experience, [Brand] is a trusted partner to industrial customers across Europe" — but provides no specific founding year, no customer count, no order volume, no facility footprint, no employee count, and no geographic specificity beyond "Europe." Per Edelman B2B Trust Barometer, vague stability claims fail the verification test that B2B procurement applies during supplier-viability evaluation. Per April Dunford's positioning framework, "decades" and "trusted partner" and "across Europe" are three claims that competitors make identically — they fail to differentiate. A specifier or procurement evaluator reading this has no anchors to compare against alternative suppliers' equally-vague claims.

**Recommendation**
Replace the vague paragraph with a specific operational-footprint section. Concrete elements:
- "Founded 1987 — 37 years in industrial supply"
- "Headquartered in Eindhoven · Offices in Düsseldorf, Lyon, Manchester"
- "1,847 active business customers across 14 European countries"
- "Annual order volume: ~58,000 orders / €47M revenue (2024)"
- "230 employees · ISO 9001 audited capacity 12,000 units/month"

Display as a stat block (4-6 numbered tiles with icons) rather than a paragraph — easier to scan in F-pattern reading. Include a small map of operational locations. Keep the numbers current (recommend updating annually after fiscal-year close). For numbers that change frequently (customer count, orders), set quarterly update cadence. Avoid suspicious rounding ("1,000+") — specific numbers ("1,847") outperform rounded numbers by signaling that the brand actually counts and reports.

**Test specification**
- **Hypothesis:** If we replace vague "decades of experience" prose with a specific scale-and-footprint stat block, then supplier-viability anxiety will decrease and downstream conversion will improve because procurement-led visitors get the verifiable anchors they need to shortlist (Edelman B2B Trust Barometer; April Dunford proof through specificity).
- **Variant A:** "Decades of experience" prose paragraph
- **Variant B:** 4-6 stat tiles with founding year, footprint, customer count, order volume, employee count
- **Primary metric:** about-us-referred conversion to product/quote engagement
- **Secondary metrics:** scroll-to-stat-block rate, time-on-page, multi-session return
- **Expected impact:** +5% to +12% on about-us-referred conversion
- **ICE:** I=6, C=7, E=8 → 7.0
- **Source:** Edelman B2B Trust Barometer 2024-2025; April Dunford "Obviously Awesome"

## Example 5: 🟠 Important — Contact, approachability & escalation paths (high-AOV technical B2B)

### 🟠 Contact & escalation — Phone number absent; contact only via web form

**Diagnosis**
The about-us page (and site footer) shows no phone number anywhere — contact is only available via a web form. For a high-AOV technical B2B (custom industrial automation, typical orders €25k+), this absence directly contradicts the trust signals the brand is trying to build elsewhere on the page (engineering depth, project capability, ISO certifications). Per MECLABS' anxiety axis, escalation anxiety is a primary B2B concern: "If something goes wrong at €25k order scale, who do I actually talk to?" Per Cialdini's liking principle, visible accessibility makes a supplier feel like a partner, not a vendor. The web-form-only contact pattern is appropriate for low-AOV transactional B2B but signals inappropriate distance for project-scale technical B2B.

**Recommendation**
Three additions:
1. **Phone number prominent**: visible in page header AND in a dedicated "Contact" section near the bottom of about-us. Show country-specific numbers if applicable ("NL: +31 40 XXX XXXX · DE: +49 XX XXXX XXXX").
2. **Office hours specified**: "Customer service Mon-Fri 8:00-18:00 CET · Technical specialists available 8:00-17:00 CET."
3. **Escalation path explicit**: for active customers, "Your dedicated account manager handles your account directly — contact details visible in your account dashboard." For prospects, "Initial inquiries handled by [Named contact, Sales Operations Director] — response within 4 business hours."

Pair the contact section with one or two named contact-people photos (sales operations lead, technical-support lead) rather than generic call-center stock imagery. The goal is signaling that real people answer real questions at real numbers.

**Test specification**
- **Hypothesis:** If we add visible phone number, office hours, and named escalation contacts, then about-us-referred quote-request conversion will increase because high-AOV technical buyers gain confidence that the supplier is reachable at project scale (MECLABS anxiety reduction; Cialdini liking through accessibility).
- **Variant A:** web-form-only contact, no phone, no named contacts
- **Variant B:** prominent phone + hours + named escalation contacts with photos
- **Primary metric:** about-us-referred quote-request rate
- **Secondary metrics:** phone-click rate, contact-form-submission rate (may decrease as phone takes share), multi-session conversion
- **Expected impact:** +8% to +18% on about-us-referred quote-request rate
- **ICE:** I=6, C=7, E=9 → 7.3
- **Source:** MECLABS Conversion Sequence Heuristic (anxiety axis); Cialdini "Influence" (liking principle)

## Example 6: 🟢 Nice-to-have — Sustainability / ESG / corporate responsibility (brand makes no ESG claims)

### 🟢 Sustainability / ESG — Category marked not applicable for this brand

**Diagnosis**
This is a small specialist B2B supplier of industrial fasteners serving manufacturing and construction. The brand makes no current ESG or sustainability claims on its site, the category is impact-neutral (no consumer-facing greenwashing risk, no clear sustainability differentiation opportunity), and the brand voice is fundamentally technical-practical rather than values-driven. Per Edelman B2B Trust Barometer's findings on ESG, **specific verifiable ESG claims outperform vague ESG claims by 4-5x — but adding ESG content without genuine substance underperforms making no claims at all** (perceived greenwashing damages trust more than absence-of-ESG-content). For this brand and category, recommending ESG additions would be forcing content that does not match brand reality.

**Recommendation**
No recommendation for ESG content additions. Instead, two notes for future consideration:
1. If procurement-led customers begin asking ESG questions in sales conversations (track this in CRM), reconsider — that signal indicates category expectations are shifting.
2. If the brand undertakes any specific ESG initiative (supplier audit program, packaging reduction, employee programs), document with metrics before publishing — even a single specific verifiable claim outperforms multiple vague ones.

This category is marked "not applicable" rather than padded with generic recommendations. Honest exclusion is better than performative inclusion.

**Test specification**
Not applicable — no change recommended.

**ICE:** Not scored — this is a category-skip finding.

**Source:** Edelman B2B Trust Barometer 2024-2025 (greenwashing detection findings); brand-fit principle

## What makes these examples high-quality

- **B2B-type-aware:** each example signals which B2B type (transactional / quote-driven / wholesale) and calibrates recommendations
- **Company-size-aware:** small specialist vs mid-sized vs corporate scale recognized in framing
- **B2B-frameworks integrated:** Edelman B2B Trust Barometer, MEDDIC, April Dunford, Cialdini appear alongside core CRO frameworks
- **Specific:** every observation names exact elements, copy alternatives, layout direction
- **Sourced:** every claim references a specific principle, study, or institution
- **Honest about lower B2B about-us test confidence:** ICE Confidence scores typically 7-8 rather than 8-9 (no Baymard B2B about-us research database to claim 9+)
- **Honest about longer sales cycle measurement:** impact estimates note 30-90 day windows
- **Honest about category-skip:** Example 6 shows how to honestly mark a category as not applicable rather than padding with weak recommendations
- **Authenticity restraint:** no example claims specific photos are stock, specific testimonials are fabricated, specific certifications are invalid — recommendations frame as "add verification details" rather than accusations
- **Permission restraint:** customer-logo recommendations explicitly note "confirmed authorized" rather than assuming
- **Test-ready:** hypothesis follows "If X, then Y, because Z"; primary metric is B2B-about-us-specific
- **Concrete copy:** exact headline alternatives, exact stat-tile elements, exact certification-verification format
- **Dutch translations natural:** when output is Dutch, "over ons", "klantreferenties", "certificeringen" used appropriately; "huren" never used

Always match this standard. If a finding can't reach this standard, either dig deeper or remove it from the audit.
