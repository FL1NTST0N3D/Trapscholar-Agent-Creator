# Frameworks for B2B Leadgen About Us Page Audits

This reference file contains the CRO and B2B-aboutus-specific frameworks, principles, and applied research. Each section maps to a finding category from the main skill.

---

## CRITICAL — Visual verification before every finding

B2B aboutus pages are uniquely prone to fabricated findings because so much depends on VISUAL signals invisible to HTML:
- Leadership-team photo quality, composition, professional polish
- Founder/CEO photo presence and authenticity
- Milestone-timeline graphics
- Award/certification badges (SOC 2, ISO 27001, Gartner, Forrester logos)
- Customer-portfolio carousels (often dynamic)
- Office-location photos and maps
- Embedded videos (founder-message, company-story)
- Press logos and analyst-recognition badges

**For every finding, verify against screenshots before delivering.**

---

## CRITICAL — Supplier-credibility is the primary conversion job

The B2B aboutus visitor is in a different state than B2C aboutus or other B2B pages:

**B2C aboutus visitor:**
- Evaluating PEOPLE for personal service
- Anxieties: warmth-fit, authentic-feel, personality-match
- Trust-building via faces, story, values

**B2B aboutus visitor:**
- Evaluating VENDOR for organizational decision
- Anxieties: vendor-risk, longevity, stability, scalability, references-quality
- Trust-building via track-record, leadership, certifications, customer-portfolio

**Implications for findings:**
- Don't recommend "more warmth-driven copy" or "founder hobbies" on enterprise B2B
- Don't recommend aggressive transactional CTAs
- DO recommend track-record evidence, vendor-stability signals, leadership-visibility, customer-portfolio depth
- DO recommend professional polish (opposite of B2C authenticity-imperfection)

## CRITICAL — Procurement-perspective drives recommendations

B2B aboutus pages are read by:
- **Champion** (sponsor doing initial research)
- **Economic buyer** (CFO/MD/budget owner) — vendor-stability check
- **Technical buyer** (IT/Engineering) — expertise/capability check
- **Procurement** (legal/finance/compliance) — vendor-due-diligence
- **End-user** (specialist who will use service) — relevance-check

Late-stage in evaluation, procurement does FORMAL vendor-due-diligence:
- Vendor financial stability (publicly visible signals)
- Compliance certifications matching their requirements
- Insurance/liability coverage
- References from peer-companies
- Years operating + key milestones
- Geographic footprint + operational capacity

The aboutus is one of the few pages designed to answer these questions.

## CRITICAL — Professional polish over authenticity (B2B-context)

For B2B aboutus, polish patterns are OPPOSITE to B2C aboutus:

**B2C aboutus (warmth-driven services):**
- Authentic imperfection builds trust
- Stock-style polish damages trust
- Founder-with-hobbies humanizes

**B2B aboutus (supplier-credibility):**
- Professional polish signals competence
- Imperfect-rough imagery may signal "small operation, can they scale?"
- Polished leadership-photos appropriate
- Production-quality video reinforces "established company"

**Calibration:**
- **Founder-led consultancy/agency:** balance shifts — some warmth OK, but still professional
- **Mid-market SaaS / consultancy:** professional polish dominant
- **Enterprise SaaS / services:** full professional polish, minimal warmth
- **Public-sector targeting B2B:** maximum formality, minimal warmth

Don't import B2C-authenticity recommendations into B2B-aboutus audits without calibration check.

## CRITICAL — Leadership-visibility (not team-wide-grid)

B2B aboutus leadership-visibility differs from B2C:

**B2C aboutus team-photos:**
- Whole team visible (especially for small service-business)
- Per-person bios with personality
- "Meet the team" framing

**B2B aboutus leadership-visibility:**
- Leadership-team prominent (CEO, founders, key C-level executives)
- NOT team-wide-grid (too many people for B2B context — visitors care about decision-makers)
- Brief professional bios
- Optional: full team page deeper

**Calibration:**
- **Founder-led consultancy/agency (small):** founder/founders + key team-members visible, similar to B2C
- **Mid-market SaaS / consultancy:** leadership team (5-8 executives) visible
- **Enterprise:** leadership team + key VP-level, full team usually elsewhere
- **Public-sector / large consultancy:** leadership prominent, full team rarely listed

## CRITICAL — Generic company-size signals only (NEVER specific financials)

Company-size signals on B2B aboutus are CALIBRATED to GENERIC only:

**Safe to recommend (publicly visible generic signals):**
- Years operating ("Sinds 2008")
- Customer-count ("200+ klanten")
- Employee-count ("75 medewerkers")
- Geographic footprint ("Actief in 12 landen")
- Vestigingen-count ("Kantoren in Amsterdam, Berlijn, London")
- Founding year + key milestones
- Industry-vertical coverage

**NEVER recommend disclosing (sensitive financials):**
- Annual recurring revenue (ARR)
- Specific revenue / omzet
- Funding-rounds, valuations
- Profit margins
- Specific customer-LTV

**Rule:** Claude does NOT recommend disclosing specific financial information unless that information is already publicly visible on the audited site. The recommendation surface stays within generic signals.

**Calibration by context:**
- **MKB-B2B and small consultancy:** typically "checked, brief observation" — generic signals less material
- **Mid-market B2B:** generic signals nuttig (employees, customers, years)
- **Enterprise B2B selling to enterprise:** generic signals important for vendor-due-diligence

## CRITICAL — B2B-service-type calibration

Recommendations vary heavily by B2B-service-type:

**B2B SaaS low-ticket (€50-500/m):**
- Lichter due-diligence-context
- Leadership-visibility lichter
- Track-record less critical
- Customer-portfolio still relevant

**B2B SaaS mid-ticket (€500-5k/m):**
- Milestones + customer-portfolio dominant
- Leadership matig
- Certifications relevant (SOC 2 minimum)

**B2B SaaS enterprise (€5k+/m):**
- Full vendor-due-diligence context
- Leadership + financial-stability + compliance + milestones all critical
- Customer-portfolio with named-customers prominent
- Awards/analyst-recognition dominant

**B2B consultancy / agency:**
- Founder-led visibility hoger
- Named-expertise per partner/director dominant
- Case-studies via customer-portfolio
- Sector-vertical expertise visible

**B2B professional services** (accountancy, legal-B2B, financial advisors B2B):
- Credentials per partner critical (BIG-registratie, advocaat-orde, AFM, RvBVA)
- Regulatoire-compliance prominent
- Named partner-team
- Year-establishment heavily emphasized

**B2B managed services** (IT, infrastructure, security):
- Security-certifications dominant (SOC 2, ISO 27001, HIPAA, PCI-DSS)
- Uptime track-record
- Customer-portfolio with case-metrics
- Technical-leadership visible

## Core frameworks (apply across the entire audit)

### Edelman B2B Trust Barometer
B2B trust on aboutus comes from:
- **Track-record longevity** ("Sinds 2008" + milestones)
- **Named leadership** (visible CEO/founders/executives)
- **Customer-portfolio** (peer-companies)
- **Third-party validation** (analyst recognition, awards, press)
- **Certifications** (SOC 2, ISO 27001, GDPR, industry-specific)
- **Vendor-stability signals** (employees, geographic footprint)

### Forrester B2B buyer behavior
Key findings applied to aboutus:
- 73% of B2B decision-makers use vendor aboutus during late-stage evaluation
- Procurement/legal often check aboutus separately from business-stakeholders
- Vendor-due-diligence checklist includes track-record, leadership, certifications, references
- Aboutus impact on decision moderate but real — most heavy-leverage at procurement-stage

### April Dunford B2B positioning (applied to aboutus)
On aboutus, positioning must support supplier-credibility:
- "Wie zijn wij voor wie?"  
- "Wat onderscheidt ons in de markt?"
- "Waarom kunnen procurement/finance erop vertrouwen dat wij hier in 3-5 jaar nog zijn?"
- "Wat is onze track-record voor klanten in jouw situatie?"

### StoryBrand framework (Donald Miller) — B2B-adapted
On B2B aboutus, customer-as-hero is calibrated:
- Customer = organization with business-challenge
- Brand = trusted vendor solving organizational pain
- Plan = proven methodology + track-record
- Aboutus copy emphasizes WHY this brand can be relied upon, not warmth-narrative

### Cialdini's principles on B2B aboutus
- **Authority** — VERY heavy on B2B aboutus. Analyst recognition, certifications, named leadership, awards.
- **Social proof** — named-customer portfolio, customer-counts, peer-validation
- **Reciprocity** — knowledge-sharing (whitepapers, sector-reports) signals expertise
- **Commitment-consistency** — published commitments (sustainability, security) build trust
- **Liking** — calibrated lighter than B2C — professional warmth, not personal warmth
- **Scarcity** — typically NOT appropriate on B2B aboutus

### MECLABS Conversion Sequence Heuristic on B2B aboutus
C = 4m + 3v + 2(i−f) − 2a

On B2B aboutus:
- **m (motivation):** procurement-driven (late-stage check, "should we proceed?")
- **v (value):** vendor-credibility value
- **i (incentive):** soft (no aggressive CTA appropriate)
- **f (friction):** missing track-record, no certifications, anonymous leadership, no customer-references
- **a (anxiety):** vendor-stability, "will they be around in 3 years?", "can they scale with us?"

### Fogg Behavior Model on B2B aboutus
Aboutus desired behavior is "feel confident enough to proceed with vendor-evaluation":
- MOTIVATION: builds through track-record evidence + leadership-trust + customer-portfolio + certifications
- ABILITY: downstream (page itself just needs to be navigable + scannable)
- TRIGGER: soft routing to next-step (demo, contact, service-page)

### Jobs-to-be-Done on B2B aboutus
B2B visitors on aboutus are doing ONE of:
1. **Vendor-credibility verification** — "Can I trust this vendor with our business?"
2. **Track-record check** — "Have they been around long enough? Doing this seriously?"
3. **Leadership-evaluation** — "Who's at the top? Are they qualified?"
4. **Customer-validation** — "Who else trusts them? Companies like ours?"
5. **Compliance-due-diligence** — "Do they have the certifications we need?"
6. **Internal-sell research** — "I need shareable proof for my buying-committee"
7. **Sales-cycle final-check** — "Last verification before contracting"

Each job needs different content. A high-converting B2B aboutus serves multiple jobs without forcing one path.

**Important Dutch translation:** never use "huren" for JTBD framing. Use "kiezen voor", "inzetten voor", "implementeren om X op te lossen".

## Category 1: Above-the-fold company-essence & supplier-credibility hook

### April Dunford B2B positioning on aboutus
Hero must answer in 5-8 seconds:
- WIE zijn wij? (company identity)
- WAT staan wij voor? (positioning)
- WAAROM kunnen klanten op ons rekenen? (supplier-credibility signal)

### Edelman B2B Trust Barometer
First impression on aboutus carries trust-formation weight:
- Specific year-of-founding ("Sinds 2008") beats vague ("Jarenlange ervaring")
- Customer-count or industry-impact in hero is strong anchor
- Named-leadership-visible in hero builds immediate authority

### Aboutus-specific hero patterns
- Brand-essence headline + supplier-credibility anchor
- Optional: founder-quote or leadership-quote
- Optional: company-introduction video (short, professional)
- Avoid corporate-poetry hero ("We help businesses unlock potential")

### Best practices
- Hero communicates WIE + WAT + WAAROM-TRUST in 5-8 seconden
- Specific credibility-anchor visible (year + customer-count + industry-focus)
- Professional imagery (no stock business-people)
- Avoid corporate-jargon ("excellence", "passion", "transform")

### Common failures
- Generic "Welcome to..." corporate-poetry
- Stock business-imagery
- Mission-vision-values wall as hero (too abstract)
- Brand-history dump (visitor not interested in detail yet)
- No credibility-anchor in hero

### B2B-service-type calibration
- SaaS enterprise: brand-essence + customer-count + analyst-recognition hero
- SaaS mid-ticket: brand-essence + customer-count + key-milestones hero
- Consultancy: brand-essence + founder-anchor + sector-focus hero
- Professional services: heritage + sector-expertise + partner-anchor hero
- Managed services: brand-essence + uptime/certifications anchor hero

## Category 2: Bedrijfsverhaal / oprichting / missie (purpose-driven)

### StoryBrand framework (B2B-adapted)
On B2B aboutus, brand-story must be customer-centered:
- Customer-pain that founded brand (specifieke business-uitdaging)
- Founder/team-expertise origin (how they qualified to solve this)
- Approach-difference (what makes this brand's solution distinct)
- Outcomes-track-record (what customers achieve)

### B2B purpose-driven narrative research
- Specific stories with dates, places, motivations outperform abstract values
- Customer-pain-centered stories outperform brand-glorification
- "Why we exist" framing builds trust if concrete
- Vague purpose-statements ("transforming industries") signal marketing-speak

### Founder-story-impact research (B2B-adapted)
- B2B brands with founder-story have 15-30% higher engagement on aboutus
- Stories with specific dates, places, motivations outperform vague framings
- Customer-impact metric (lives changed, problems solved) outperforms inward-focused

### Best practices
- Concrete story with dates, places, motivations
- Customer-pain at center (organizational, not personal)
- Founder/leadership accountability visible
- Mission/values emerge FROM story, not stated abstractly
- Story length 200-400 words optimal
- Optional: photos illustrating story moments

### Common failures
- Abstract mission-vision-values walls
- "Our journey of excellence" corporate-poetry
- No founder/leadership mentioned by name
- Story focuses on brand growth, not customer impact
- Generic founding date without context

### B2B-service-type calibration
- Founder-led consultancy: founder-story dominant
- Mid-market SaaS: company-story balanced
- Enterprise SaaS: company-story + multiple-leader-quotes
- Professional services: heritage-story (when long-established)
- Managed services: founding-story + technical-expertise-origin

## Category 3: Company milestones & track record

### B2B longevity-as-credibility research
- Years operating = primary vendor-stability signal
- Visible timeline of growth-moments builds credibility
- Customer-count milestones (100th, 500th, 1000th customer) provide narrative anchors
- Geographic expansion milestones signal scale

### Vendor-stability signals (Edelman B2B Trust Barometer)
Specific milestones that drive vendor-trust:
- Founding year
- First-major-customer milestone
- Funding/investment milestones (only if already publicly announced)
- Geographic expansion (new market entry)
- Product/service expansion
- Customer-count milestones
- Employee-count milestones
- Industry awards / recognition years

### Track-record narrative patterns
- Visual timeline (vertical or horizontal) outperforms text-paragraph
- Key dates + brief context per milestone
- Visual icons or graphics per milestone
- 5-10 milestones optimal (under 5: thin; over 10: dilutes)

### Best practices
- Visible timeline graphic on aboutus
- Founding year prominent
- 5-10 key milestones
- Customer-count + employee-count + geographic milestones
- Recent milestones (last 1-2 years) visible to signal active growth

### Common failures
- No milestones or timeline visible
- Generic "established years ago" without specific year
- Stale milestones (last update 3+ years ago) — signals stagnation
- Bullet-list without visual timeline
- Self-aggrandizing milestones ("our team grew to amazing strength")

### B2B-service-type calibration
- SaaS enterprise: timeline + customer-milestones + funding-milestones (if public) + analyst-recognition
- SaaS mid-ticket: timeline + key customer-milestones
- Consultancy: timeline + named-client milestones + sector-expansion
- Professional services: heritage-timeline (often longer)
- Managed services: timeline + certifications + customer-retention milestones

### Authenticity restraint
A finding may recommend verifying milestone-authenticity. A finding may NOT claim specific milestones are fabricated without strong evidence.

## Category 4: Customer-portfolio & named clients

### Cialdini social proof in B2B (aboutus-context)
On aboutus, customer-portfolio differs from servicepage/homepage logos:
- More detailed (full list of named-customers, not just logo-strip)
- May include industry-tags or use-case-tags
- Optional: detailed case-study links per client
- Aggregate counts more prominent

### Customer-logo credibility research
On aboutus specifically:
- Full customer-portfolio (15-50 logos) acceptable
- Industry-tagged or use-case-tagged for scanning
- "Our customers" page link if portfolio is extensive
- Customer-count aggregate ("200+ companies trust [Brand]")

### Forrester named-customer impact
- B2B decision-makers cite named-customer-portfolio as #2 trust-factor on aboutus
- Industry-relevance critical (peer-companies dominate)
- Named-customer permission-context: contractually-OK customers visible

### Best practices
- Customer-portfolio section visible on aboutus
- 15-50 named-customers (more depth than homepage)
- Industry/sector-tagging for filtering
- Customer-count aggregate visible
- Optional: link to detailed case-study per major customer

### Common failures
- No customer-portfolio on aboutus
- Generic "happy customers" without specifics
- Mix of customer + partner logos (visitor confused)
- Outdated customer-list (3+ years no updates)
- Anonymous "Fortune 500 financial services" without naming

### Authenticity restraint
A finding may recommend verifying customer-logo permission. A finding may NOT claim logos are unauthorized without strong evidence.

### B2B-service-type calibration
- Enterprise SaaS: extensive named-portfolio with industry-tags
- Mid-market SaaS: 20-30 named-customers
- Consultancy: detailed customer-portfolio + named-case-studies
- Professional services: named-client list (sector-tagged)
- Managed services: customer-portfolio + uptime-track-record per major customer

## Category 5: Leadership team & key expertise

### Edelman B2B Trust Barometer (named leadership)
Named leadership signals:
- Specific accountability ("name + role + bio")
- Recognizable expertise ("ex-McKinsey", "ex-Google", "ex-[relevant industry]")
- Sector-credentials
- LinkedIn-link per leader for verification

### Cialdini authority on aboutus leadership
- Credentials specific and verifiable
- Years experience visible per leader
- Specialisations named (not generic "expert")
- Awards / recognitions per individual where applicable

### B2B leadership-visibility research
- Named leadership lifts aboutus engagement 20-40%
- LinkedIn-link per leader builds verification-trust
- Brief bios (50-100 words) outperform extensive paragraphs
- Photo quality matters (professional, not stock)

### Best practices
- Leadership-team (CEO, founders, key C-level) visible by photo + name + role
- Professional photos (consistent style)
- Brief bios (50-100 words) per leader
- Optional: LinkedIn-link per leader
- Optional: specialisation/sector-expertise per leader
- Optional: founder-quote or message

### Common failures
- Anonymous leadership ("our experienced team")
- Stock-style identical-pose photos
- Bios all-corporate ("X is passionate about Y") with no specifics
- Founder hidden (especially problematic for founder-led brands)
- LinkedIn-links absent (verification difficult)

### Authenticity restraint
A finding may recommend verifying photo authenticity. A finding may NOT assert specific photos are stock without strong evidence.

### B2B-service-type calibration (key)
- Founder-led consultancy/agency: founder/founders prominent, full bio
- Mid-market SaaS: leadership-team (5-8 executives)
- Enterprise SaaS: leadership-team (6-10 executives) + VPs
- Professional services: partner-team prominent (each with credentials)
- Managed services: technical-leadership + business-leadership balanced

### NOT B2C team-wide-grid
Important distinction: B2B aboutus shows LEADERSHIP, not the whole team. B2C aboutus often shows whole team (especially small service-businesses). B2B aboutus showing 30+ team-photo-grid is rarely appropriate — full team usually on separate /team or /careers page.

## Category 6: Awards, analyst recognition & press

### Cialdini authority via third-party
B2B third-party authority signals:
- Industry awards (relevant to vertical)
- Analyst recognition (Gartner Magic Quadrant, Forrester Wave, IDC MarketScape)
- Press mentions in B2B-relevant publications
- Speaking engagements / conference recognition

### Gartner/Forrester recognition impact
For SaaS/managed-services specifically:
- Gartner Magic Quadrant placement is high-impact trust-signal
- Forrester Wave equivalent
- IDC MarketScape, G2 Grid, Capterra recognition (lichter)
- Recent year more impact than dated recognition

### B2B press-credibility patterns
- Press-logos with publication-name + year (when relevant)
- Industry-publications more relevant than mainstream
- "As featured in" framing with logos
- Link to article ideal but not always feasible

### Best practices
- Awards/analyst-recognition section visible
- Recent recognitions prominent (last 1-3 years)
- Industry-relevant only
- Press logos with year + publication-name
- Optional: links to articles or analyst reports

### Common failures
- No awards/recognition section despite having
- Generic award-badges without context
- Stale awards (5+ years old, no recent ones)
- Press-logos from irrelevant publications
- Self-claimed awards without third-party verification

### B2B-service-type calibration
- SaaS enterprise: analyst-recognition critical
- SaaS mid-ticket: G2/Capterra recognition + press
- Consultancy: industry awards + speaking engagements
- Professional services: industry-publications + sector-awards
- Managed services: technical-awards + customer-satisfaction-recognitions

## Category 7: Certifications, compliance & security

### Vendor-due-diligence requirements
B2B procurement evaluates aboutus for compliance:
- **SOC 2 Type II** — security audit baseline for SaaS
- **ISO 27001** — information-security management
- **GDPR / AVG compliance** — EU privacy
- **HIPAA** — healthcare US
- **PCI DSS** — payment-card industry
- **Industry-specific** — depends on vertical (FINRA for finance, FedRAMP for US gov, etc.)

### B2B procurement-readiness research
- Visible certifications reduce procurement-friction 30-50%
- Self-claimed compliance (without certifications) signals lower trust
- Compliance section dedicated outperforms scattered badges
- Recent audit-dates visible (within 1-2 years)

### Best practices
- Dedicated compliance/security section on aboutus
- Certification badges with year + auditor where possible
- Industry-relevant only (no overkill)
- Link to compliance-report or security-overview where feasible
- Recent recertifications visible

### Common failures
- No compliance section despite having certifications
- Generic certification logos without context
- Self-claimed compliance ("GDPR-compliant" without certification)
- Outdated certifications (expired audits)
- Mismatch between target-market and certifications

### B2B-service-type calibration
- SaaS enterprise: full compliance section critical (SOC 2 + ISO 27001 + GDPR baseline)
- SaaS mid-ticket: SOC 2 + GDPR usually sufficient
- Consultancy: less applicable (no formal certifications); industry-membership instead
- Professional services: regulatoire-compliance (BIG, AFM, NBA, advocaten-orde) prominent
- Managed services: full security/compliance suite + industry-specific

### Authenticity restraint
A finding may recommend verifying certification-authenticity. A finding may NOT claim certifications are invalid without strong evidence.

## Category 10: CTA-routing naar conversie

### Soft-CTA patterns on B2B aboutus
B2B aboutus is NOT a primary conversion page. CTAs should be:
- Soft (not aggressive)
- Contextual (woven into content)
- Multi-path (route to different next-pages)
- Late-stage-appropriate (visitor is verifying, not browsing)

### Aboutus-as-trust-routing (B2B-context)
The aboutus job is to BUILD trust, then route:
- "Ready to evaluate? → [Demo / Contact-sales]"
- "Curious about specific service? → [Service-page]"
- "Want to talk to leadership? → [Contact us]"
- "Considering us as vendor? → [Procurement-info]"

### Multi-path B2B CTAs
- End of leadership-section: "Connect with our team →"
- End of customer-portfolio: "See detailed case studies →"
- End of certifications: "Download our security overview →"
- After milestones: "Discuss your needs →"

### Best practices
- Multiple soft CTAs throughout page (not just bottom)
- Contextual to surrounding content
- Action-oriented but not aggressive
- Route to relevant next-page
- Optional: sticky-mobile CTA
- Footer-CTA as safety net

### Common failures
- No CTAs on aboutus (visitor reaches end with no next-step)
- Single aggressive CTA (mismatch with trust-building atmosphere)
- Generic "Contact us" at footer only
- Multiple competing aggressive CTAs
- CTAs disconnected from surrounding content

### B2B-service-type calibration
- SaaS: "Request demo" + "Talk to sales" + "Download security overview"
