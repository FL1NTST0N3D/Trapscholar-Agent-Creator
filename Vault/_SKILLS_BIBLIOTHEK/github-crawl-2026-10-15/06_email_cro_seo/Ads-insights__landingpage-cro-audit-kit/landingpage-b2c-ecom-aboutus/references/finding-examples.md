# Worked finding examples — about us page quality calibration

These examples show what a high-quality about us page finding looks like across different categories, brand snapshots, and ICE scores. Use them as a reference standard. Findings should match this depth and specificity — not be vaguer, not be more generic.

---

## Example 1: 🔴 Critical — Above-the-fold positioning & promise (mid-market lifestyle brand)

### 🔴 Above-the-fold positioning — Generic "Welcome to [Brand]" headline wastes prime real estate

**Diagnosis**
The page opens with a hero image and the headline "Welkom bij [Brand]". The subhead reads "Wij zijn gepassioneerd over hoogwaardige producten en uitstekende service." Per Donald Miller's StoryBrand framework, the most-read sentence on any about us page is the headline above-the-fold — and this headline answers neither "why should I keep reading?" nor "what does this company do for me?". The visitor learns nothing about who the brand serves, what makes it different, or why their problem matters. Per Nielsen Norman, ~80% of about us visitors scan rather than read; a generic opening loses them before they reach the substance. WiderFunnel's LIFT framework identifies this as a relevance failure: the page doesn't immediately confirm to a referred visitor that they're in the right place.

**Recommendation**
Replace with a customer-focused headline that names the audience and what the brand does for them. Example structure: "[Brand] helpt [audience] met [outcome] zonder [common pain]." Concrete options to test:
- "Een rustige plek vinden voor wie je liefhad — daar zetten wij ons sinds 2015 voor in." (premium memorial brand)
- "Kantoorinrichting waar je vakkundige team trots op is — zonder showroom-prijzen." (mid-market office furniture)
- "Eerlijke kleding gemaakt in Portugal, gedragen door 47.000 mensen die het verschil voelen." (sustainable fashion)

Keep the headline under 15 words. Subheadline (10-15 words) can name the brand and clarify the category. Visual: keep current hero if it's authentic team/founder imagery; replace if it's stock.

**Test specification**
- **Hypothesis:** If we replace the generic "Welkom"-headline with a customer-focused promise headline, then about-us exit rate will decrease and click-through to category/product pages will increase because visitors immediately understand who the brand serves and why they should keep reading (StoryBrand framework; WiderFunnel LIFT relevance factor).
- **Variant A:** "Welkom bij [Brand]" + generic passion subhead
- **Variant B:** Customer-focused promise headline + brand-clarifying subhead
- **Primary metric:** about-us exit rate
- **Secondary metrics:** scroll depth past the fold, click-through to category/product, multi-session conversion
- **Expected impact:** -8% to -18% on about-us exit rate
- **ICE:** I=8, C=8, E=10 → 8.7
- **Source:** Donald Miller StoryBrand framework; WiderFunnel LIFT relevance factor; Nielsen Norman about us research

## Example 2: 🔴 Critical — Founder/team presentation (Dutch artisan brand)

### 🔴 Founder/team presentation — Brand is anonymous; no founder or team visible

**Diagnosis**
The about us page describes the company's story, values, and craft philosophy in detail but shows no founders, no team members, no faces anywhere. The only imagery is product close-ups and workspace shots without people. Per Cialdini's liking principle and Edelman's Trust Pyramid, faces are the foundation of about-us trust — visitors who don't see the people behind a brand perceive it as anonymous and risky. This is especially impactful for small/artisan brands where the human story is the primary differentiator from larger competitors. Edelman's 2024 research consistently shows founder-visible brands outperform anonymous brands on trust metrics, particularly in categories where craft, expertise, or values-alignment matter.

**Recommendation**
Add a "De mensen achter [Brand]" section midway through the page containing:
- Founder/owner photo (warm, professional but not corporate-glossy — workshop or workplace setting works well)
- Name + role + 2-3 sentence story (what brought them to this work, what they care about)
- If multiple key people (designer, head craftsman, customer service lead): include each with photo + name + role
- Optional: handwritten signature, video greeting (30-60 seconds), or "Een berichtje van [Founder]" quote block

Style: authentic over polished. Photos by a local photographer outperform expensive studio shots in trust impact (Edelman finding). Maintain brand visual consistency — same color treatment as product photography.

**Test specification**
- **Hypothesis:** If we add a "De mensen achter [Brand]" section with founder/team photos and brief stories, then about-us exit rate will decrease and time on page will increase because visitors see the humans behind the brand and resolve the anonymity anxiety (Cialdini liking principle; Edelman Trust Pyramid).
- **Variant A:** No team or founder visible on page
- **Variant B:** Dedicated team section with photos + names + roles + brief stories
- **Primary metric:** about-us exit rate
- **Secondary metrics:** time on page, scroll depth past team section, click-through to category/product
- **Expected impact:** -6% to -15% on about-us exit rate
- **ICE:** I=8, C=8, E=6 → 7.3 — *Edit: Critical-tier because the impact is foundational, but ease is lower due to photography production. Marginal call between Critical and Important; placed Critical because the absence is severe.*
- **Source:** Cialdini's liking principle; Edelman Trust Barometer / Trust Pyramid

## Example 3: 🔴 Critical — Brand story & narrative arc (premium specialty retailer)

### 🔴 Brand story — Chronological "Founded in [year]" history buries customer relevance

**Diagnosis**
The brand story opens with "Onze geschiedenis begint in 2008, toen [founder] besloot om..." and continues as a chronological timeline through 2010, 2015, 2018, 2022. The story is well-written but it's a company-history CV, not a customer-focused narrative. Per Donald Miller's StoryBrand framework, the brand should position itself as the **guide**, not the **hero** — the customer is the hero of the story. This chronological format puts the brand in the hero position ("look at our journey") and buries the question every visitor is asking: "why does this matter to me?". Nielsen Norman's F-pattern research shows visitors scan and bail when the first 50 words don't establish relevance. The customer's problem and the brand's empathy with that problem appear nowhere in the first 200 words.

**Recommendation**
Restructure the story using StoryBrand narrative arc:
1. **The problem we saw** — what frustrated us / our customers (1-2 sentences)
2. **The insight that changed things** — what we realized was missing (1-2 sentences)
3. **The solution we built** — what we did differently (2-3 sentences)
4. **The impact today** — what's changed for customers (1-2 sentences with concrete proof)

Concrete opening to test (adapt to actual brand context): "Te veel [audience] kopen [product category] op basis van prijs, en zijn er binnen een jaar van af. Wij wilden [products] maken die je nog generaties lang bij je houdt." Then describe how the brand built that solution.

Keep total story to 200-400 words. The chronological timeline can become a small "Mijlpalen" sidebar or be removed entirely.

**Test specification**
- **Hypothesis:** If we restructure the brand story from chronological history to StoryBrand narrative arc (problem → insight → solution → impact), then time on page and scroll depth will increase because the story immediately answers "why should I care?" and pulls the visitor through (StoryBrand framework; Nielsen Norman F-pattern).
- **Variant A:** Chronological "Founded in 2008..." timeline narrative
- **Variant B:** StoryBrand structure opening with customer problem → brand insight → solution → impact
- **Primary metric:** time on page
- **Secondary metrics:** scroll depth past the story section, about-us exit rate, click-through to category/product
- **Expected impact:** +12% to +25% on time on page; -4% to -10% on exit rate
- **ICE:** I=8, C=7, E=8 → 7.7
- **Source:** Donald Miller StoryBrand framework; Nielsen Norman F-pattern eye-tracking; Jobs-to-be-Done

## Example 4: 🟠 Important — Proof & credibility signals (mid-market Dutch webshop)

### 🟠 Proof & credibility signals — Customer count vague; no press, awards, or third-party verification

**Diagnosis**
The page mentions "duizenden tevreden klanten" without a specific number, "jarenlange ervaring" without specifying years, and references reviews without linking to a verifiable source. Per Spiegel Research Center, verifiable proof signals with specific numbers, dates, and sources outperform vague claims by 3-5x in conversion impact. Per Cialdini, authority and social proof are most credible when stacked with independent verification (third-party certifications, press mentions, named awards). The current page contains the **claim** of credibility but none of the **evidence**. Visitors evaluating trust scan for verifiable signals and find nothing they can independently check.

**Recommendation**
Add a "Wat anderen over ons zeggen" or "Bewijs van vertrouwen" block containing:
- Specific customer count with timeframe ("12,847 bestellingen sinds 2018" beats "duizenden klanten")
- WebwinkelKeur or Kiyoh rating with public link ("9.4/10 — bekijk alle 1,247 reviews →")
- Press logos with year and brief context ("Featured in De Volkskrant 2023: 'Authentic Dutch craft revival'")
- Industry certifications relevant to category (OEKO-TEX, FSC, B-Corp, Thuiswinkel Waarborg, etc.)
- KvK number and physical address as background legitimacy signal

Position the block after the brand story (trust is built, evidence reinforces it). Keep visual treatment subtle — third-party logos in muted treatment, not aggressive sale-banner styling.

**Test specification**
- **Hypothesis:** If we replace vague proof claims with specific, verifiable proof signals (named press, dated customer count, linked rating), then about-us exit rate will decrease and click-through to category/product will increase because trust-evaluating visitors find evidence they can independently verify (Spiegel Research Center; Cialdini authority + social proof).
- **Variant A:** Vague proof claims ("duizenden klanten", "jarenlange ervaring")
- **Variant B:** Specific proof block with numbers, dates, sources, and verifiable third-party logos
- **Primary metric:** about-us exit rate
- **Secondary metrics:** click-through to category/product, click-through to review platform, multi-session conversion
- **Expected impact:** -3% to -9% on about-us exit rate
- **ICE:** I=6, C=7, E=8 → 7.0
- **Source:** Spiegel Research Center on verifiable claims; Cialdini's authority and social proof principles

## Example 5: 🟠 Important — Customer voice & testimonials (premium artisan brand)

### 🟠 Customer voice — Anonymous text quotes with no photos, names, or context

**Diagnosis**
The page displays three customer quotes ("Geweldige producten en service!", "Aanrader!", "Top kwaliteit"), each attributed to a first name and initial ("Marije H.", "Jan V.", "Sandra B."). No photos, no specific product mentions, no story arc. Per Spiegel Research Center, specific, named, photo-attributed testimonials outperform anonymous quotes by 5-7x in trust and conversion impact. Per Cialdini, social proof works hardest when the visitor can identify with the testimonial-giver — first-name-and-initial attributions create distance and trigger skepticism (visitors mentally categorize them as "might be made up"). The current testimonials add little; for a premium brand they actively cheapen the page because the format reads as standard ecom-template rather than authentic story.

**Recommendation**
Replace the three anonymous quotes with 2-3 substantive testimonial stories containing:
- Customer photo (with permission, ideally taken in their own context — home, workplace, with the product)
- Full name + brief context ("Marije van der Heide, hospice-medewerker in Utrecht")
- Specific product or experience ("Voor mijn moeder kozen we de glazen urn na een gesprek met...")
- Story arc — what they were looking for, how they discovered the brand, how the product fit
- Optional: short video testimonial (30-60 seconds) for the strongest story

Quality over quantity: two real, specific, photo-attributed stories outperform ten anonymous quotes. For a premium brand the testimonials should match the brand's editorial voice — controlled, considered, never sales-y.

**Test specification**
- **Hypothesis:** If we replace anonymous text quotes with 2-3 photo-attributed customer stories with names and context, then time on page and click-through to product will increase because visitors find identifiable proof they can relate to (Spiegel Research Center; Cialdini social proof).
- **Variant A:** Three anonymous text quotes with first-name-initial attribution
- **Variant B:** Two or three photo-attributed customer stories with full names, context, and product-specific narrative
- **Primary metric:** time on page
- **Secondary metrics:** click-through to product, scroll depth past testimonial section, multi-session conversion
- **Expected impact:** +8% to +20% on time on page; +3% to +9% on click-through to product
- **ICE:** I=6, C=7, E=5 → 6.0
- **Source:** Spiegel Research Center; Cialdini's social proof principle; Nielsen Norman testimonial research

## Example 6: 🟢 Nice-to-have — Cross-links to conversion paths (mid-market specialty)

### 🟢 Cross-links to conversion paths — Page ends in dead-end with no CTA

**Diagnosis**
The about us page ends after the final paragraph of the brand story with no CTA, no cross-link to product or category pages, no newsletter signup, no contact prompt. Per WiderFunnel's LIFT framework, the clarity factor is unfulfilled: visitors who finished reading have built trust but have no clear next step. Per Fogg's Behavior Model (B = MAT), the conditions are met (motivation high after trust-building, ability available) but the **trigger** is missing — there's no signal of what to do next. Visitors either navigate back to homepage (losing the about-us-built momentum) or bounce. Note that this is Nice-to-have because the impact is moderated by traffic volume (about us is 1-3% of total traffic), but it's a low-effort fix with clear upside.

**Recommendation**
Add a closing section after the brand story containing:
- Clear CTA aligned with brand voice ("Bekijk onze collectie" for product-led, "Vind een product voor jouw situatie" for complex categories, "Stuur ons een berichtje" for service-led)
- Optional: 3-4 featured product cards under "Onze meest geliefde producten" — visual continuity into shopping intent
- Secondary path: link to FAQ, shipping, or returns for visitors still resolving anxiety
- Newsletter signup AFTER the CTAs (not before), framed as ongoing relationship not lead capture

Keep the closing visually subtle — about us is a trust page, not a sales page. The CTAs should feel like natural next steps, not sales pitches.

**Test specification**
- **Hypothesis:** If we add a closing section with clear CTA and curated product highlights, then click-through from about us to category/product pages will increase because visitors with built trust have a clear, motivated next step (WiderFunnel LIFT clarity factor; Fogg Behavior Model trigger).
- **Variant A:** Page ends after brand story with no CTA or cross-links
- **Variant B:** Closing section with primary CTA + 3-4 product cards + secondary links (FAQ, shipping)
- **Primary metric:** click-through rate from about us to category/product pages
- **Secondary metrics:** about-us exit rate, multi-session conversion, newsletter signup rate
- **Expected impact:** +15% to +35% on click-through from about us (note: high relative impact, but on small absolute traffic volume)
- **ICE:** I=4, C=7, E=9 → 6.7 — *Marginal between Important and Nice-to-have; placed Nice-to-have because absolute traffic volume on about us limits revenue impact even with large relative gains.*
- **Source:** WiderFunnel LIFT framework (clarity factor); Fogg Behavior Model (B = MAT); Jobs-to-be-Done

## What makes these examples high-quality

- **Specific:** every observation names an exact element with position and behavior
- **Sourced:** every claim references a specific principle, study, or institution
- **Brand-aware:** recommendations consider whether they fit the brand snapshot (premium vs mid-market vs value, company size, values footprint)
- **Honest about uncertainty:** when data is missing, the finding says so and recommends research, not action
- **Test-ready:** hypothesis follows "If X, then Y, because Z"; primary metric is named (about us metrics: exit rate, time on page, click-through, multi-session conversion); ICE is justified by the breakdown
- **Concrete copy and behavior:** not "improve the story" — actual headline structures, exact testimonial formats, specific CTA copy
- **Honest about confidence:** about us audits average lower confidence than PDP/cart due to smaller public test base; this is acknowledged in scores
- **Honest about absolute impact:** relative gains can be large but absolute traffic volume on about us is small, so expected revenue impact is moderated — calls this out when relevant
- **Dutch translations natural:** when output is Dutch, JTBD framing avoids "huren"; terms like "over ons", "ons verhaal", "het team" used naturally; English brand frameworks named (StoryBrand, Edelman) when no Dutch equivalent exists

Always match this standard. If a finding can't reach this standard, either dig deeper or remove it from the audit.
